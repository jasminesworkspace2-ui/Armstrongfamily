Rev. George W. Ballard

Rev. George W. Ballard was born September 4 1861 — the son of Newton Ballard and Rebecca Ballard — in Smith or Upshur County Texas. He became an ordained minister and lived the second half of his life in Waco, McLennan County Texas. He owned property at 724 East Johnson Street, Waco — recorded in McLennan County deed records Volume 289 Page 655. That property was lost to a tax sale on November 7 2017 under Cause No. 2014-3167-4 — more than 80 years after Rev. Ballard's death.

He married Mary Ann Edwards Ballard — born November 4 1870 Texas — died March 19 1949 Fort Worth. Mary Ann's parents have been identified through AncestryDNA ThruLines as Benjamin Edwards born 1848 and Bettie Edwards born 1849 — but this has not been confirmed by primary source document.

Rev. George W. Ballard did not know his parents' exact origins. His parental birthplace data varied across four census years — a documented pattern for formerly enslaved people and their children who did not have reliable records of where their parents were born. This is not an error — it is an honest reflection of what was lost during enslavement.

James Wilson Ballard Sr.

James Wilson Ballard Sr. was born August 26 1897 in Palestine, Anderson County, Texas — the son of Rev. George W. Ballard and Mary Ann Edwards Ballard. He married Eva Armstrong Ballard — daughter of Hellen Nichols Armstrong and John Armstrong of Bellville — uniting the Ballard line from East Texas with the Nichols-Armstrong line from Austin County.

James Wilson Ballard Sr. spent his final years as a patient at Austin State Hospital in Austin, Texas — confirmed by the 1950 Federal Census. He died on March 13 1953 — his daughter Margie Nell's twenty-fourth birthday. The date is its own kind of record.

Newton Ballard

Newton Ballard is the foundation of the Ballard family line — the formerly enslaved man whose freedom claim on 160 acres of Upshur County Texas in 1856 is the earliest independent primary source record connecting this family to freedom. Everything else in the Ballard research builds from him.

Newton Ballard was born approximately 1830 in Mississippi — almost certainly in Copiah County or Washington County Mississippi — the counties where Lewis F. Ballard Sr. held land and lived before migrating to Texas. He was enslaved by Lewis F. Ballard Sr. in Smith County Texas by 1850 — appearing as an unnamed male approximately age 20 born Mississippi in Ballard's slave schedule on Page 458.

On August 26 1856 Newton Ballard filed a land claim for 160 acres in Upshur County Texas — Texas GLO File No. 3378 · Survey No. 124 · Abstract 131. That date — August 26 — is the same calendar date as James Wilson Ballard Sr.'s birth year in 1897 — forty-one years apart. Newton named his son George Washington Ballard — born September 4 1861 — who confirmed Newton as his father on his 1937 McLennan County death certificate.

Newton Ballard's land went delinquent on Upshur County tax rolls by 1886 — Abstract 131 appearing as unrendered and unclaimed in 1886 and again in 1891. He was almost certainly dead or gone from Upshur County by the early 1880s. His death date, death location, and burial place remain unconfirmed by any primary source document found to date.

Research Context · The Enslaver's Records Carry Newton's History

The White Ballard Family

In African American genealogy research the paper trail of the enslaving family is often the only vehicle that carries records of the enslaved person. The white Ballard family's land patents, tax assessments, census records, and slave schedules are the documents that confirm Newton Ballard existed before emancipation. This section documents that family as research context — not as ancestors — but as the framework that preserved Newton's history.

Important Framing

Lewis F. Ballard Sr. and his family are documented here because their records are the primary sources for Newton Ballard's pre-emancipation history. They are not your ancestors. They are the people whose records confirm your ancestor existed.

The Copiah County Mississippi Community

Lewis F. Ballard Sr. lived in Copiah County Mississippi in 1841 — confirmed by the state census. The Copiah County deed records from 1825 document the community he was part of — the Byrd family, the Boyd family, the Blount family, and the Smiley family all appear in Copiah County deed records in the 1820s. The same Byrd and Boyd families appear in the 1855 Upshur County Texas tax assessment alongside the Ballard family — confirming these were connected planter communities that migrated together from Mississippi to Texas.

The name Newton appears in Copiah County Mississippi deed records as early as August 31 1825 — when Margaret Byrd transferred a nine-year-old enslaved boy named Newton to her daughter. This Newton is too old to be your Newton Ballard — born approximately 1815-1816 versus Newton Ballard born ca. 1830 — but confirms the name Newton was used for enslaved people in Copiah County in the generation before Newton Ballard's birth.

Davenport-Ballard Connection · Upshur County 1855

Charles Davenport appears in the 1855 Upshur County Texas tax assessment holding a full league — 4,428 acres — in the same county and year as Lewis F. Ballard Sr. The Davenport family gave Patience to Patty Waldrup in Newberry County South Carolina in 1795. A Davenport in Upshur County Texas in 1855 alongside the Ballard family confirms the geographic overlap of these two enslaving family networks in East Texas — the same two families that appear at the origins of both your maternal and paternal lines.

Unconfirmed Hypothesis · Clearly Labeled · August 2026

The Louisa Hypothesis

⚠ Hypothesis Status — Not Confirmed by Primary Source

The Louisa Hypothesis proposes connecting Newton Ballard to an enslaved woman named Louisa from the John Bullard estate in Ouachita Parish Louisiana. This is a research hypothesis — not a confirmed fact. It is presented here clearly labeled as such. Nothing in this section should be added to the family tree or described as confirmed ancestry.

What the Documents Show

Two Ouachita Parish Louisiana deeds — one from 1839 and one from 1856 — both signed by John T. Faulk — connect to the Bullard family of Louisiana and document enslaved people including a woman named Louisa.

The 1856 deed — dated July 30 1856 — names John T. Sheppard and his wife Nancy Bullard — daughter of the late John Bullard — transferring property that included enslaved people from the John Bullard estate. Nancy Bullard had inherited an estate labor force from her father. In May 1854 John T. Sheppard sold a stake in this property to John T. Faulk without Nancy's consent — the 1856 deed corrected this title defect for $2,000.

The enslaved people documented in the John Bullard estate deed include Louisa · Hester · Jesse · Green · Texana · Harriett · and an unnamed infant child. The official recorder was Benjamin D. Sheppard acting as Parish Recorder and Ex-Officio Notary Public for Ouachita Parish Louisiana.

Why This Hypothesis Exists

The hypothesis connects to Newton Ballard through two threads. First — the Bullard/Ballard surname variant. The John Bullard family of Ouachita Parish Louisiana shares a surname variant with the Ballard family that enslaved Newton — Bullard and Ballard are the same name spelled differently across records of this era. Second — the geographic corridor. Louisiana to East Texas was a well-documented migration path for both free and enslaved people in the 1840s-1860s. The Louisa from John Bullard's estate could theoretically have been sold or moved to Texas after the estate was dispersed — which would place her in the same East Texas corridor where Newton Ballard was living after emancipation.

The J.T. Sheppard who appears in the 1867 Texas voter registration in Bell County — confirmed on Line 1 registered July 17 1867 — may be the same John T. Sheppard from the 1856 Louisiana deed, which would confirm the Louisiana-to-Texas migration of this family network.

What Has Not Been Confirmed

No primary source document has been found connecting Louisa from the John Bullard Louisiana estate to Newton Ballard. No marriage record, no shared census entry, no deed, no church record, no Freedmen's Bureau record connects these two people. The hypothesis is supported by circumstantial geographic and surname evidence — not by a primary source chain of custody.

Researcher Norris Douglas was contacted to search Anson County North Carolina Ballard/Bullard probate records that might provide a connecting link. Those results are still pending.
