Research Summary · What the Decade Cost This Community
Lost to Bank Crisis (confirmed): At least three people were lost — the unnamed male age 18 from the deed, and the two unnamed adult males age 24–35 from the census. L.F. Ballard Sr. carried $11,736 in simultaneous bank debt: the Union Bank Stock Mortgage ($8,200, May 1839), the Grand Gulf Bank multi-mortgage ($3,060, April 1840), and the Bank of Port Gibson default judgment ($476.41, March 1840). Adult males of working age were the highest-value liquidation assets.
Judy's fate: Named in the 1840 deed but absent from the 1850 Texas schedule. Age 32 by 1850 — still prime working age. She was either sold at the September 1845 courthouse foreclosure or in a separate transaction. Her name does not reappear in any confirmed Ballard record in Texas.
Scylla's survival: The 1840 census/deed discrepancy is unresolved, but the naming of Newton's granddaughter Silla Ballard in the 1870 Upshur County census strongly suggests Scylla survived, traveled to Texas, and remained significant enough in the community's memory that her name was carried forward two generations.
Mary Ballard's courthouse rescue: On September 15 1845, Mary Ballard purchased the entire 517-acre plantation back for $917 at the courthouse door auction — preventing further liquidation of the enslaved community. Without that intervention, Newton and the remaining members of this community would likely have been sold and scattered.
Open Research Questions
Smith County Texas probate records 1850–1860 — L.F. Ballard Sr. estate — would name Newton individually and document the transfer of enslaved people after his death.
Copiah County Deed Book G Pages 385–386 — the 1839 Union Bank Stock Mortgage front pages — may name Newton as an infant and identify his birth mother.
Copiah County Deed Book B Page 117 — Smith neighbor indemnity bond — may name the two unnamed adult males lost to the bank crisis.
Judy's fate — no record found post-1845 — courthouse auction purchaser records from September 15 1845 may identify who acquired her.
Scylla/1840 census discrepancy — only one female age 24–35 in census vs. two women (Judy and Scylla) in that bracket in the deed — requires resolution.
Rains County Grantee/Grantor Index 1873–1885 — where Newton went after selling his Upshur County land for 250 gold dollars in August 1873.
