For Family Members · Plain Language

This is a list of every person confirmed in this research — both ancestors in the direct line and other enslaved people found in the same historical records. If you are searching for a family member and you know they may have been in Bellville, Texas or Newberry County, South Carolina — look here first. Every entry has a source so you can verify it yourself.

This registry documents every enslaved person confirmed in the primary source research for this archive. It is offered as a public resource to other researchers searching for their own ancestors. Every entry is confirmed by a primary source document. Nothing is invented or inferred.

Enslaved people were systematically excluded from the official record. Their names — when recorded at all — appear in the documents of the people who enslaved them: slave schedules, estate inventories, probate partitions, deed transfers, and church histories. This registry consolidates every such record found in the course of building this archive.

If your ancestor was enslaved in Austin County Texas, Smith County Texas, Upshur County Texas, Copiah County Mississippi, Washington County Mississippi, Wilcox County Georgia, Halifax County Virginia, or Newberry County South Carolina — this registry may help you find them. Every document cited here is accessible through FamilySearch, the Texas GLO, Ancestry, or the county courthouses listed in the Paper Trail.

Highlighted rows indicate confirmed ancestors of this family. All others are members of the same enslaved communities — people whose descendants may be searching for them.

Research Database · Public Resource

People first. Records first. Connections earned.

This Registry is broader than the Armstrong direct line. It preserves named and unnamed enslaved people recovered from the same deeds, probate files, slave schedules, court records, church histories, bills of sale, and other historical sources. A person's presence here does not mean they are an Armstrong ancestor. The database is designed so another descendant can use the record trail without inheriting an unsupported conclusion.

How to read this database

Search results are research aids, not automatic family conclusions. Matching names, ages, locations, values, or migration routes can generate a lead but do not establish identity by themselves. The Registry preserves distinctions when the evidence does not justify a merge.

Thomas Bell / John G. Bell Estate Bellville, Austin County, Texas · 1854–1880

Thomas Bell (b. ca. 1796–1802 North Carolina, d. 1858 Bellville) was one of Stephen F. Austin's original colonists and co-donor of the land that became the town of Bellville. His adopted son, John Gideon Slade, took the name John Gideon Bell upon adoption (deed dated January 4, 1854) and became executor of Thomas Bell's estate. This section documents the enslaved people named in Thomas Bell's 1858 will and the household later recorded in the 1860 slave schedule under John G. Bell — the same man later confirmed running a Bellville hotel where a 12-year-old girl surnamed Bell appears as a domestic servant in the 1870 census. No document has yet confirmed a blood relationship between anyone in this household and Rendy Blayn or Easter Bell Nichols. It is documented here because the surname Bell — carried throughout the Bellville freedperson community, including by Rendy Blayn on Easter's death certificate — very plausibly originates from households like this one.

Dupree–Groce Chattel Mortgage Austin County, Texas · 1851

Lewis G. Dupree mortgaged eight enslaved people to Leonard W. Groce on February 22, 1851, as collateral for a $1,000.80 loan, recorded in Austin County Deed of Trust/Mortgage Book B, pages 42–43. No name overlap with the confirmed family line has been found. Documented here as evidence of how enslaved people were routinely used as loan collateral in this exact Austin County community, and because a receipt attached to this document confirms A.J. Bell as Sheriff of Austin County by 1851 — corroborating the Bell family's decades-long hold on Austin County civil offices later described in an 1869 Freedmen's Bureau report (see We Are Bellville).

Jim & Fannie George Justice Precinct 1 (Bellville), Austin County, Texas · 1880

Documented here not as part of the confirmed direct family line, but because this household holds one of the most direct statements of African ancestry found anywhere in this archive. Jim George, head of household, and his wife Fannie George both have a father's birthplace recorded plainly as Africa — one generation closer to the Middle Passage than almost any other record collected here. Jim George's own age is recorded as 117, almost certainly an enumerator's rough estimate rather than a literal fact — a common distortion for elderly Black Americans whose birth was never formally recorded — but the African birthplace statement for both his and Fannie's fathers stands independent of that error. Fannie George's birth year (~1810) and location closely match "Fanny," age 55, named in John Nichols's 1864 Bellville estate partition — a real, worth-noting coincidence, but not yet confirmed as the same woman by any document naming a direct connection.

A note for researchers

This database intentionally contains people who are not part of the confirmed Armstrong line. Their inclusion means the archive encountered them in a relevant historical record—not that a family relationship has been established. Use the record trail as a starting point, and verify identity against the original document before extending a lineage.

These names were recorded
as property. They were people.

Every name on this page belonged to a real person. Most of them have no further record. This registry is offered in the hope that someone, somewhere, is searching for them — and finds them here.
