DOCUMENTED HEIR · DOWNSTREAM EVIDENCE ATTACHED ONLY WHEN PERSON-SPECIFIC
Common documentary anchor

1839 · Rankin County

The distribution instrument is the starting point for this dossier. It recites the earlier 1795 gift of Patience to Martha “Patsy” Davenport Waldrop and names Patience's increase, including Clarissa, Rachel, Tom, William, Amanda and Harriet. This heir's exact allotment must be extracted from the instrument rather than inferred.

Primary-source control

Rankin County · Deed Book 4 · p. 435

March 25, 1839. The archive's reconstruction preserves the deed's family relationships as Patience → Rachel → Tom and Patience → Clarissa → William, Amanda, Harriet. The eight Waldrop children belong to the legal distribution layer. The recovered transcription names all eight children together as grantees and conveys Patience and her named descendants to them collectively. This dossier therefore does not assign any one named enslaved person exclusively to this heir without a separate later partition, sale, or other person-specific record.

Post-1839 separation trail

Estate interests must be reconstructed deed by deed

The archive records Claiborne purchasing interests from other Waldrop heirs after the joint 1839 conveyance. Those transactions are potentially the legal mechanism by which jointly held interests became concentrated, but the dossier will not state which share—or which member of Patience's family—passed to Claiborne until the underlying deeds are attached and transcribed.

1860–1861 · Sabine Parish

The archive carries a lead to the 1860 U.S. slave schedule for C. P. Waldrop in Sabine Parish. A separate 1912 parish history lists “C. P. Waldrup” among residents who owned six or more enslaved people in 1861. The history is secondary evidence; the schedule image and Louisiana probate/land records need image-level review before unnamed schedule entries are assigned identities.
