The Ballard Paternal Line

Eva's husband's family · How the Ballard line connects

Eva Armstrong married James Wilson Ballard Sr. — connecting the Nichols-Armstrong line from Bellville to the Ballard line from East Texas. James Wilson Ballard Sr. was the grandson of Newton Ballard — a formerly enslaved man who filed for 160 acres of land in Upshur County Texas on August 26 1856 while his enslaver was still alive. That act of claiming land is the founding document of the Ballard family's freedom.

A Note on the Waldrop DNA · Why Some Family Members Carry European Ancestry

AncestryDNA results for this family show 16–22% European DNA. This did not come from choice. It came from the Waldrop household — where Patience and her daughter Clarissa were held as enslaved people from 1795 through emancipation. Patience was held by the Waldrop family for over 40 years across South Carolina and Mississippi. The European DNA in this family's ancestry traces to that household — to assault, not to ancestry anyone chose.

Molley — Patience's mother — was in the Sims household in Union County South Carolina. The Waldrop DNA did not enter the line through Molley. It entered through Patience's children and grandchildren who were born and raised in the Waldrop household. This is documented history, not speculation. It is part of the record because the record should be complete.
