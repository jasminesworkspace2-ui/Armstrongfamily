Norman

Caswell Corbin

February 10, 1921 · Cleveland, Ohio — April 21, 1974 · Santa Ana, California

He called himself a people's person. He had survived things that would have broken most men. Fifteen hundred people came to his funeral to confirm what kind of man he had become on the other side of that survival.

scroll
Primary source confirmed · Ohio · Kentucky · Michigan · 1921–1955

Before Santa Ana

Norman Caswell Corbin was born on February 10 1921 in Cuyahoga County, Ohio — Cleveland. His mother was Mattie Corbin — confirmed as next of kin on his 1942 WWII draft card — who later became Mattie Lou Simpson of Santa Ana. His father is not identified in any record found to date.

By 1930 Norman was nine years old and living in Paris, Bourbon County, Kentucky — in the household of his uncle Albert Bacon, age 44. The 1930 census lists him as a nephew. His sister Helen R. Bishop would later live in Kentucky at the time of his death — the family's roots in Bourbon County Kentucky ran deep, even though Norman was born in Ohio.

By 1940 Norman was twenty-three years old and living in Detroit, Ward 3 — in the household of his cousin Ernest Smith. By February 1942 he was still in Detroit, working for A.S. Beck. His draft card describes him as six feet tall, 145 pounds, light brown complexion, black hair, brown eyes.

On November 11 1942 Norman Caswell Corbin enlisted in the United States Army — Company I, 365th Infantry. He was discharged on July 23 1943 as Private First Class, Service Number 36 543 588. He had served eight months. The 365th Infantry was a Black unit — part of the 92nd Infantry Division, known as the Buffalo Soldiers — serving in a segregated Army in a war being fought for freedom.

By 1950 Norman was twenty-nine years old, back in Detroit, married, and working as a clothing finisher. His first wife at that time was Ophelia Mae Corbin — born February 7 1929 in Michigan. They worked together as servants at the home of Harry J. Bloch at 509 North Rodeo Drive in Beverly Hills, California.

October 2 1955 · Los Angeles · Confirmed by California Death Index and newspaper records

October 2, 1955

On the morning of October 2 1955 Norman Caswell Corbin was driving his wife Ophelia to St. Matthias Episcopal Church at 1901 West Washington Boulevard in Los Angeles. His car accelerated to approximately 75 miles per hour, struck a curb and an ornamental light standard, and came to a stop four feet from the church door. Ophelia Mae Corbin — age 26 — was killed instantly, pinned to her seat. The crash was so violent the front wheel was jammed through the floorboards. Firemen had to extricate her body.

Norman was in critical condition — possible skull fracture and broken ribs. He was hospitalized. He was also booked on suspicion of manslaughter.

The criminal case against Norman Caswell Corbin was eventually dismissed for lack of witnesses. He was cleared of all charges.

Norman survived. He was hospitalized in Los Angeles — severely injured, in critical condition, fighting to recover from a crash that had killed his wife. The manslaughter proceedings continued in the courts for some time after the crash. Family oral history confirms he was dealing with the criminal situation for years after Ophelia's death.

Family Oral History · Not Confirmed by Primary Source · Consistent with Primary Source Record

According to family oral history, Margie Nell Ballard met Norman Corbin while he was hospitalized — she was working as a candy striper or volunteer at the hospital. He was in a coma or severely injured at the time. That is how they found each other.

This account is not confirmed by any primary source document but is consistent with the confirmed newspaper record — Norman was hospitalized in critical condition in Los Angeles in October 1955 following the crash.

ca. 1958 – April 21 1974 · Santa Ana, California

Southwest Santa Ana

Norman Caswell Corbin arrived in Orange County sometime in the late 1950s. He settled in the Black community of southwest Santa Ana and never left. He married Margie Nell Ballard — the granddaughter of Easter Bell Nichols, the great-great-granddaughter of Patience. Their first child together was born in 1951 according to family records — Michael — though the full sequence of their children across the years suggests their relationship began before the formal marriage. Hjordis L. Corbin was born January 20 1960.

They lived first at 617 Shelley Street and later at 622 South Townsend Street, Santa Ana. Norman was a non-denominational minister, a newspaper publisher, a school district liaison, an NAACP vice president, and a city council candidate. He published the Orange County Star Review — a weekly newspaper. He served as intercultural specialist for the Santa Ana Unified School District. He served as vice chairman of the Orange County Human Relations Commission. He ran for Santa Ana City Council Ward 2 in the April 1971 election as a Democrat.

He founded the Sunset Little League and the Senior Citizens Southwest Community Center. He served on the Santa Ana College Community Relations Advisory Council. He was active in Head Start and the PTA. He was photographed by a newspaper handing the keys to the first subsidized housing unit in Santa Ana to a family named Hernandez — the Human Relations Commission making good on its name.

He carried no life insurance. His riches were not in the form of money. When someone's welfare check was late he gave away his last dollar. When someone was hungry he fed them. When people came to him with problems at two in the morning he answered the door.

"People with problems came to him seven days a week at all hours of the day and night. He gave them the feeling that everything would be well, a feeling of security. He didn't stop until he'd done his best to solve their problems."

— Margie Nell Corbin · quoted in Orange County newspaper · 1974

The Poem in His Desk

After Norman died the people who cleaned out his desk at the Santa Ana Unified School District found a poem he had written. The newspaper published it in his obituary. It is the only piece of confirmed writing in his own words. He titled it The Success Family.

Norman Caswell Corbin · Found in his desk after April 21 1974 The Success Family
The father of success is work.
The mother of success is ambition.
The oldest son is common sense.
Some of the other boys are perseverance,
honesty, thoroughness and cooperation.

The oldest daughter is character.
Some of her sisters are cheerfulness,
loyalty, courtesy, care,
economy, sincerity and harmony.
The baby is opportunity.

Get acquainted with the old man
and you will be able to get along
pretty well with all of the family.

Yesterday is gone and belongs to eternity.
Tomorrow may never come and is God's.
Published in Orange County California newspaper obituary · April 1974

The journalist who covered his death — Richard Buffum — wrote: "I'm sorry I didn't know him. Good men like him are hard to come by anywhere. I just thought you'd like to know that Norman Corbin was here."

April 21 1974 · Santa Ana California

April 21, 1974

In April 1974 Norman traveled to Kentucky to visit a sick aunt. He became ill while he was there. He was brought to Riverview Hospital in Santa Ana and died on April 21 1974 of pneumonia. He was fifty-three years old. He was buried at Fairhaven Memorial Park in Santa Ana — plot: Lawn South — Find A Grave Memorial No. 58638768.

He carried no life insurance. Margie was left with seven children — the youngest was eight years old, the oldest was twenty-one.

The community responded. A six-hour benefit jazz and rock concert — billed as a Conglomeration of Stars — was held at the City Hall Annex on Santa Ana Boulevard at Ross Street. Every cent went to Margie and the children and to establish a scholarship fund in Norman's name. Performers included Art Robinson and Soul Providers, Johnny Adams, Hank Alexander, North Wind, Black Gold, Just Funk, Bertha Eason, Billy Higgins, and others.

1,500 people came to his funeral. Visitation was held at Winbigler Family Mortuary on North Grand Avenue. The funeral service was at Jerome Park, 2100 West McFadden. Interment followed at Fairhaven Memorial Park.

What Remains

Fifty years after Norman Caswell Corbin died, his name is still on a building in Santa Ana.

The Corbin Family Resource Center — located at 2215 West McFadden Avenue, Suite G, Santa Ana 92704 — operates today offering Medi-Cal and CalFresh application assistance, legal advocacy, life skills workshops, youth art workshops, and a Youth Volunteer Leadership Group. It serves the same southwest Santa Ana community Norman spent fourteen years building.

Norman Corbin had no life insurance. He left behind seven children and a widow. He left behind a community that named a building after him. He left behind a poem about work and ambition and character that a newspaper published for all of Orange County to read.

He left behind Hjordis. And Hjordis left behind Jasmine. And Jasmine built this archive — carrying the full story of who this family is, where it came from, and what it survived — all the way back to Patience in South Carolina in 1795.
