DOCUMENTED HEIR · PERSON-SPECIFIC EVIDENCE ONLY
Common documentary anchor

1839 · Rankin County

The distribution instrument is the starting point. It recites the 1795 gift of Patience to Martha “Patsy” Davenport Waldrop and names Patience's increase, including Clarissa, Rachel, Tom, William, Amanda and Harriet. This heir's exact allotment must be extracted from the instrument rather than inferred.

Primary-source control

Rankin County · Deed Book 4 · p. 435

March 25, 1839. The archive's reconstruction preserves the deed's family relationships as Patience → Rachel → Tom and Patience → Clarissa → William, Amanda, Harriet. The eight Waldrop children belong to the legal distribution layer. The recovered transcription names all eight children together as grantees and conveys Patience and her named descendants to them collectively. This dossier therefore does not assign any one named enslaved person exclusively to this heir without a separate later partition, sale, or other person-specific record.

Evidence discipline

What the archive will not assume

Documented fact, supported connection, research lead and unresolved question remain separate. Family relationship, shared surname, nearby household or migration route does not by itself prove ownership or movement of an enslaved person.

Evidence gap

What is still missing

The present archive establishes Wiley as one of the eight heir destinations, but does not yet supply a person-specific named enslaved-person transfer for this dossier. The gap is displayed rather than filled by inference.

UNRESOLVED · DO NOT INFER
January 15, 1849 · recorded November 28, 1849

Wiley B. Waldrop → Claiborne P. Waldrop

The reviewed record shows Wiley transferring to Claiborne, for $220, “all of my interest in Est. of Hezekiah Waldrop & Patty his wife.” The instrument was later recorded in Sabine Parish. This is direct evidence that at least one heir's estate interest was consolidated into Claiborne's hands. The wording transfers Wiley's estate interest; it should not be expanded into a claim that a particular member of Patience's family was transferred unless the instrument itself names that person.
