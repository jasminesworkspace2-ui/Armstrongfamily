01 · Scope of the file

A record of people, property, and relationships.

This file follows Francis Davenport and the Davenport records in which enslaved people are named, transferred, inventoried, inherited, or otherwise appear. The purpose is not to collapse every person with the Davenport name into one household, but to preserve the documentary trail and identify where continuity is established, where it is only possible, and where the record remains open.

02 · Francis Davenport

Family identification.

Family reconstruction places Francis Davenport in the Davenport family descending from Isaac Davenport Sr. and Phoebe Eva Sawyer. The supplied research also documents four marriages associated with Francis, while noting unresolved gaps in the family reconstruction.

Father
Isaac Davenport Sr.

Born 1693; died 1749. Married Phoebe Eva Sawyer. The supplied FamilySearch reconstruction lists seven children.

Subject
Francis Davenport

Born 1718; died 1803. The Davenport record associated with the 1795 Newberry conveyances naming Patience and other enslaved people.

Primary research subject
Marriage record
Ann Whaley

Marriage dated July 20, 1740, Campbell, Albemarle County, Virginia. The supplied family reconstruction attributes ten children to this marriage.

Later marriage
Mary Young · Mary Turner

Mary Young appears as a later wife in Newberry research. Mary Turner appears as the widow of William Turner and is the wife named in Francis's 1798 will in connection with Peter.

Research caution

The supplied FamilySearch record itself carries a research alert. The Virginia marriage, later North Carolina land trail, Newberry residence, four-marriage reconstruction, and parentage of Martha “Patty” Davenport require the distinctions already preserved in the source material.

03 · Deeds & conveyances

August 1795: the names in the record.

Newberry County Deed Book C records a concentrated series of Davenport conveyances in August 1795. These entries are the documentary foundation for the Davenport–Waldrop connection and also preserve the names of enslaved people who went into other Davenport households.

Aug. 1, 1795
Deed C:369

Francis Davenport Sr. → Nancy Waldrop

An enslaved person was conveyed to Nancy Waldrop. The surviving research identifies the person as unnamed in the record under review. Nancy is therefore a documented recipient; the person's later identity remains unresolved.

Newberry County, SC · Deed Book C · p. 369 · Aug. 1, 1795
Aug. 4, 1795
Deed C:367

Francis Davenport Sr. → Patty Waldrop

The deed identifies one enslaved girl named Patience. Witnesses: Isaac Davenport, Edward Turner, and Elizabeth Towling. The deed was proved before Charles Griffin, J.P., on August 5.

Record language preserved: “Francis Davenport, Sr., to daughter Patty Waldrop, gift, one negro girl named Patience.”
Aug. 4, 1795

The supplied transcription describes a larger conveyance of slaves and household goods to Isaac Davenport, William Davenport, David Davenport, Sarah Beeks, and Abigail Davenport.

04 · Enslaved people index

Names preserved beyond the direct family line.

The archive records these people because they appear in Davenport documents, not only because they connect to Patience. A future researcher may be looking for any one of these names.

Patience
1795 · Patty Waldrop

Named in Deed Book C:367 as an enslaved girl conveyed by Francis Davenport Sr. to his daughter Patty Waldrop.

Rachel
1795 · Isaac Davenport

Named in the larger August 1795 distribution. Do not merge this Rachel with Patience's later daughter without documentary continuity.

Fanny / Jenny
1795 · Isaac Davenport

The supplied record preserves the name as Fanny (or Jenny). Exact reading and later continuity remain research questions.

Peter
1795 · 1798

Named in the 1795 distribution to Isaac and again in Francis Davenport's 1798 will in connection with Mary Turner. Whether these references are the same man remains unresolved.

Coy · Mingo · Dick · Oliver · Tom · Nan
1795 distribution

Each is named in the August 1795 Davenport distribution and preserved here as an independent research subject.

Milly · Selay · Jack
1798 · Isaac Davenport

Owen Flinn conveyed Milly and her children Selay and Jack to Isaac Davenport for $550. The record explicitly identifies Selay and Jack as Milly's children.

Easter
1792 · Davenport record

Francis Davenport conveyed an enslaved girl named Easter to James Davenport in December 1792. Later Davenport/Burton records create a separate Easter–Bluford research trail.

Bluford
1829 · 1842

Jonathan Davenport purchased Bluford in 1829; Jonathan's 1842 will identifies Bluford as the son of Easter. This is a documented enslaved-family relationship.

05 · Probate

Francis Davenport's 1798 will.

The will provides a second legal record after the 1795 distribution and is especially important because it names Peter in a life-estate provision for Mary Turner.

“...wife Mary, to have Negro Peter, farm animals, household furniture, life estate...”

Newberry County, South Carolina · Wills D:40 · January 23, 1798
Peter — continuity question

The 1795 distribution names Peter among persons conveyed to Isaac Davenport. The 1798 will separately names an enslaved man Peter for Mary. The two references should not be merged as fact until a documentary bridge is found.

06 · Davenport property network

Separate transactions. Related legal world.

The Davenport record extends beyond Francis's household. These transactions are included because they establish other enslaved families and legal relationships involving Davenport descendants and associated families.

Sept. 19, 1798
Deed D:64–65

Owen Flinn → Isaac Davenport

For $550, Owen Flinn conveyed Milly, a woman; Selay, her child; and Jack, her child to Isaac Davenport. Witnessed by Sarey Waldrop; proved by David Waldrop October 17; recorded November 12, 1798.

Family group explicitly stated: Milly → Selay and Jack
Jan. 15, 1846
Estate sale

John G. Greeves estate · Claiborne County, Mississippi

Hamilton Cooper purchased seven enslaved people for $3,210: Jack, Sing, Patience, Little Dick, Hiram, Auguster, and Madison, an infant. This is a separate Patience and a separate Jack unless continuity is established.

Do not merge: shared names do not establish identity.
07 · Easter & Bluford

An enslaved family documented across records.

This research track is separate from Patience's line. It is retained because the Davenport records provide an unusually important continuity clue: a later will explicitly states a parent-child relationship.

Easter
1792 · Francis Davenport → James Davenport · enslaved girl named Easter
Easter → Bluford
1829 · Davenport/Burton estate records · continuity requires careful record-by-record reconstruction
Bluford
1842 · Jonathan Davenport's will identifies Bluford as “son of Easter”
Documentary significance

The 1842 parent-child statement is treated as stronger evidence of relationship than an age match, repeated surname, or proximity alone. The 1792-to-1829 continuity remains a separate question.

08 · Jonathan Davenport

The 1842 will and enslaved family groups.

Jonathan Davenport's will is one of the most important Davenport records in the research set because it identifies enslaved people in family relationships rather than merely as unnamed property.

Bluford

Clause 6 identifies Bluford as “son of Easter” and provides for him to James M. Davenport.

Sarah & children

Clause 7 names Sarah and her children Solomon, Elvira, Admiral, and Muzilla, together with old Sam and his wife Esther.

Martha & Anderson

Clause 4 identifies Martha and Anderson in a distribution involving Mary, Nancy, and Emily.

Albert & Nelson

Clause 8 records Albert and Nelson in a separate distribution, alongside Dorcas, Ann, and Peggy.

Primary source: South Carolina Court of Equity, Newberry District · FamilySearch Image Group 008300158 · Images 430–431 · Jonathan Davenport will, 1842
