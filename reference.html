Common Confusions · Confirmed Corrections

⚠ Confusion #1 — Two Different Women Named Helen/Hellen Hellen Nichols Armstrong ≠ Helen Nichols Reese

Hellen Nichols Armstrong — born September 1870 · Easter Bell's daughter · married John Armstrong · Eva's mother · last confirmed 1930 Fort Worth as Helen Gilbert · death date UNKNOWN · death cert NOT yet located

Helen Nichols Reese — born ca. 1884 · Easter Bell's YOUNGER daughter — SEPARATE PERSON · died December 5 1931 Bellville · cause: Pellagra · TX cert No. 54237 · informant: Eva Ballard · buried Jeff Cemetery · husband Albert Reese

TX cert 54237 belongs to Helen Reese — NOT Hellen Armstrong. These are two different daughters of Easter Bell.

⚠ Confusion #2 — Easter Bell's Mother Was Rendy, Not Clarissa Easter Bell descended from Rendy Blayn — NOT from Patience or Clarissa

Easter Bell's mother was Rendy Blayn — confirmed by Easter's 1935 death certificate listing mother's maiden name as Rendy Blayn.

Clarissa connects to the family through Joseph Nichols — Easter's husband — not through Easter herself.

The two lines merged when Joseph married Easter on December 23 1869. They were two separate enslaved families who happened to be held in the same John Nichols household in Bellville.

⚠ Confusion #3 — William Is Rachel's Son, Not Clarissa's The 1839 Rankin County deed is explicit

The 1839 Rankin County deed text reads: "Rachel's son William and Amanda and Harriet children of Clarissa."

William belongs to Rachel's line. Clarissa's confirmed children are Amanda and Harriet only.

Any document or previous research listing William as Clarissa's child is incorrect.

⚠ Confusion #4 — Clarissa Ann Morgan Is NOT Your Clarissa Two different women named Clarissa in the same Austin County community

Your Clarissa — born ca. 1807 South Carolina · Patience's daughter · died ca. early 1870s Sabine Parish Louisiana · last record as Clarey Godwin 1870 · she never went to Texas

Clarissa Ann Morgan — born ca. 1841-1846 Texas · daughter of Louisa Morgan born 1810 Virginia · married David Armstrong 1868 Austin County TX · died January 24 1918 Fort Worth · she is John Armstrong's mother — Hellen's mother-in-law

These are two entirely different women. The shared name Clarissa is coincidence.

⚠ Confusion #5 — Two John Nichols Families in Bellville 1860 Your John Nichols vs the Louisiana-born John Nichols

Your John Nichols — born ca. 1799 Mississippi · married Fanny Waldrop Oct 14 1837 · received 4428-acre land grant 1830 · died March 3 1864 · slave schedule page 032 · 9 enslaved people

Other John Nichols — born ca. 1797 Louisiana · wife T Nichols age 58 · children T.E., S.H., M.C. — completely different family · NOT connected to your research

Always verify by checking wife's name. Your John Nichols' wife was Fanny/Nancy Waldrop.

⚠ Confusion #6 — Easter Bell's Birth Year Is 1838, Not 1848 Confirmed by primary source death certificate

Easter Bell's death certificate — State File No. 54496 — states birth as July 4, 1838.

Previous AI research used ca. 1847-1848 based on a slave schedule reading. That was wrong.

She was freed at age 27 — not 17. She lived 97 years — not 87. She married at age 27.

⚠ Correction — Oscar Nichols Death Certificate

Oscar Nichols' 1949 death certificate listed Clara Alexander as his mother's maiden name — suggesting Easter Bell was Clara Alexander. This is an informant error by Walter Nichols (Oscar's son). Clara Alexander Nichols was Oscar's wife — not his mother. Easter Bell Nichols is confirmed as Oscar's mother by four other death certificates (Allen, Willie, Helen Reese, Clara Nazy) and by the 2006 tax suit. Oscar's mother was Easter Bell. Clara Alexander was his wife.

The Bellville Nichols Freedmen Community · Abstract 73

After emancipation in 1865, formerly enslaved people from the John Nichols household established themselves on portions of Abstract 73 — the John Nichols Survey — the same land where they had been held in bondage. Tax assessment rolls confirm at least four Black Nichols freedmen holding land on Abstract 73 within years of Juneteenth. Two marriages were solemnized in the same week of December 1869 — both officiated by the same minister — Rev. J. Shirl — confirming a tight-knit community legalizing relationships that had existed during enslavement.

⚠ Important Notes on 1880 Household

Hellen Nichols absent — Hellen would be approximately 9-10 years old in June 1880 but does not appear in this household. She is confirmed as Easter Bell's daughter by the 2006 tax suit naming her as co-heir. Her biological relationship to Joseph Nichols has not been confirmed by a primary source census record placing her in his household as a child. She was not born yet when the 1870 census was taken (born September 1870 · census taken June 1870).

Mulatto racial designation — the entire household is designated Mulatto by the enumerator — consistent with European admixture showing in DNA results at 16-22% across multiple calculators.

Joseph's mother born Tennessee — new primary source finding. Joseph's father born Texas · mother born Tennessee. The Waldrop family migration path from South Carolina through Tennessee to Texas explains how an enslaved woman born in Tennessee came to be in the John Nichols household in Bellville.

Easter's age underreported — Easter recorded as age 33 born ca. 1847 in the 1880 census. Her confirmed death certificate gives birth July 4 1838 making her 42 in 1880. Age underreporting was common among formerly enslaved women.
