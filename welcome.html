Seven years of research traced our family back to 1795. What I found will change how you see every name you already know.

Let me start with what you already know — and walk you backward.

You
You — reading this right now Corbin. Ballard. Armstrong. Whatever name you grew up with — this is your family's history too.

Rendy Blayn · born ca. 1817 Easter Bell's mother. Held as enslaved property in Bellville, Texas. Her surname traces to the white family that enslaved her. She was born in Arkansas or North Carolina — the two records conflict and we may never know which is correct.

7
Clarissa · born ca. 1807 · South Carolina Rendy's mother. Confirmed in a South Carolina deed from 1807 and a Mississippi deed from 1839. She traveled with the Waldrop family from South Carolina to Mississippi — not by choice.

8
Patience · born ca. 1765 · South Carolina Clarissa's mother. On August 4 1795 a man named Francis Davenport gave Patience to his daughter Patty Waldrop as property. That transaction was recorded in a deed book. That deed book survived. That is why we can trace this family all the way back to 1795.

9
Molley · born ca. 1750–1770 · West Africa or South Carolina Patience's mother. The oldest ancestor we can reach. Born somewhere in West Africa or South Carolina — the DNA points to the Yoruba and Dahomey region of West Africa, the Bight of Benin. Before her the records end and only the DNA speaks.
A Letter to the Family
From Jasmine M. Lee · 2026

Why I Built This

When I started this research in 2019 I did not know my great-great-grandmother's name. I did not know that she had been born enslaved on July 4 1838 in Bellville, Texas — that she had lived ninety-seven years — that she had buried children and outlived a husband and watched her granddaughter Eva move to California and build a new life. I did not know any of this because nobody had written it down. The silence was not an accident. It was what happened to Black families in America for two hundred years.

You know the name Corbin. You know the name Ballard. Maybe Armstrong. But do you know that behind those names is a woman named Easter Bell Nichols who was born enslaved on July 4 1838 and lived ninety-seven years? Do you know that behind Easter Bell is a woman named Patience who was given as property to a white woman in South Carolina in 1795 — and that the deed recording that transaction still exists, and that it is the reason we can trace this family at all?

Seven years later I can tell you her name. I can tell you when she was born and when she died. I can tell you that her mother was a woman named Rendy Blayn. I can tell you that her husband Joseph Nichols registered to vote on January 27 1868 — three years after emancipation — he could not read, he could not write, and he voted anyway. I can tell you that Easter Bell held legal title to a piece of the same land she was born enslaved on for 140 years after emancipation — and that in 2006 Austin County took it for unpaid taxes.

I built this archive because our family deserves to know where we come from. Not the simplified version. The real one — with the names of the people who were enslaved and the names of the people who enslaved them, with the documents and the gaps and the things we still do not know honestly labeled. This is not a celebration of how far we have come. It is a record of who was here.

Every fact on this site was confirmed by a primary source document — a census record, a death certificate, a deed, a slave schedule, a marriage license, a land patent, a voter registration, a church history, a newspaper article, or a sworn court testimony. When something has not been confirmed I say so. When something is a hypothesis I label it as a hypothesis. This family's history is extraordinary enough without invention.

This archive is for you. For Hjordis and her children. For Sonsery and Deanna and Se'Taun and Ronnie and Orngiel. For the cousins and the grandchildren and the people who will come after all of us. The silence stops here.

Jasmine M. Lee Founder & CEO · Dock Ellis Foundation · jlee@dockellisfoundation.com
