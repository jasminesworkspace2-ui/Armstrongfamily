Level 2 · The Archive's Family Map

The family tree is not just a chart. It is the working map.

This page shows how the people in the Armstrong Family Archive fit together, what the records establish, and where the reconstruction is still being tested. A solid line means the documentary relationship is established. A dashed line means the connection remains a research lead or open question.

How to read this tree

Evidence comes before certainty.

This archive does not erase a person because the documentary bridge is missing. Instead, the tree shows the difference between what a record establishes and what the evidence suggests. That lets the family story remain visible without turning a hypothesis into a fact.

The direct line

From Easter to Jasmine

The Bellville branch becomes the clearest documented path into the modern Armstrong family. Earlier generations and collateral branches remain part of the research map, but they are not presented with more certainty than the records allow.

Easter Bell Nichols
July 4, 1838 – December 17, 1935 · Bellville, Texas
Documented

Easter's 1935 death certificate identifies her as the daughter of Rendy Blayn. Post-emancipation records place Easter and Joseph Nichols in Bellville and document their marriage and family.

Earlier generations

Two research branches. One meeting point.

The earlier archive research contains two important branches. They should be visible because they guide the investigation—but they should not be collapsed into a single proven migration story.

Branch A

Molley → Patience → Clarissa

This branch is rooted in South Carolina and Mississippi records. Patience is documented in South Carolina and later in the Waldrop network; the 1839 deed is evidence of a property transfer, not proof that her descendants traveled to Texas.

Branch B

Rendy Blayn → Easter Bell

Easter's 1935 death certificate names Rendy Blayn as her mother. Rendy's own pre-emancipation origin and identity in the 1860 schedule remain research questions; the archive keeps that uncertainty visible.

Working reconstruction

Joseph Nichols + Easter Bell Nichols

The marriage of Joseph and Easter is a documented meeting point for the Texas family. The possible connection between Joseph and the Patience/Clarissa branch remains a research reconstruction, not a proven Texas migration from that branch.
