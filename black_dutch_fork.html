Where this family begins

Before Texas. Before Mississippi. Before the Waldrop migration. This family begins in a region of South Carolina called the Dutch Fork — in the households of German and Swiss immigrant farmers who brought enslaved people to the South Carolina upcountry in the 1700s. This is where Molley was. This is where Patience was given away. This is where Clarissa was born. The DNA still carries it.

Geography · South Carolina Upcountry What Is Dutch Fork?

Dutch Fork is the region of South Carolina between the Broad River and the Saluda River — two rivers that converge near present-day Columbia. It was settled in the 1740s and 1750s primarily by German and Swiss immigrants — the "Dutch" in Dutch Fork is a corruption of Deutsch, the German word for German. These were not English colonists. They were German Lutheran and Reformed Church families who received land grants from the colonial government and built farms in the South Carolina upcountry, far from the tidewater plantations of Charleston.

The counties that make up Dutch Fork include Newberry County, Laurens County, Lexington County, and parts of Union County. These are the exact counties where Molley, Patience, and Clarissa are documented in primary source deed books from 1793 through 1839.

The German families of Dutch Fork brought enslaved people to the upcountry from the coastal lowcountry — and they bought and sold enslaved people among themselves, recorded in the deed books that survive in county courthouses today. The Waldrop family, the Davenport family, the Sims family, the Barksdale family — these are German-descended Dutch Fork families whose deed records are the documentary foundation of this entire archive.

Our Family · In the Dutch Fork Records What Black Dutch Fork Means for This Family

Molley appears in a Union County South Carolina mortgage document in 1793 — the westernmost edge of the Dutch Fork region. She is listed alongside her seven children including Patience and Tom. The 1793 deed index for Laurens and Newberry Counties confirms her name at page 118 — multiple deed book entries across multiple counties.

Patience was given to Patty Waldrop on August 4, 1795 in Newberry County South Carolina — Deed Book C pages 367–368. The Waldrop family were Dutch Fork settlers. Patience was born in their household. She grew up in the Dutch Fork upcountry.

Clarissa was born ca. 1807 in South Carolina — confirmed as Patience's daughter. She grew up in the Dutch Fork community before the Waldrop family moved west to Mississippi in the 1820s and 1830s, taking their enslaved community with them.

The term Black Dutch Fork — not a historical term but a way of naming what happened — refers to the Black community that was built by and within the German immigrant households of the Dutch Fork region. Enslaved people held by Dutch Fork families were part of that community. Their descendants carry that history in their DNA and in the surnames that appear in this archive.

Primary Source · The Foundational Document · August 4, 1795
Deed Book C: 367–368 · Newberry County, South Carolina
"Francis Davenport Sr. to Patty Waldrop, his beloved daughter — one enslaved girl named Patience — August 4, 1795 — certified Charles Griffin J.P. — recorded August 18, 1795."

This deed — recording the transfer of a human being as property — is the foundational document of this entire archive. Without it, this family's history before 1865 would be unrecoverable. It was executed in the Dutch Fork region of Newberry County, South Carolina.

DNA · What the Science Confirms The DNA Evidence of Dutch Fork Origins

The AncestryDNA results for this family confirm what the documents show — and add what the documents cannot say. The DNA results show ancestry consistent with both West African origin (Yoruba and Dahomey regions — what is now Nigeria and Benin) and European ancestry consistent with the German and Swiss settler communities of the Dutch Fork.

The European DNA in this family is not coincidental. It is the biological record of what happened in the Dutch Fork households where Molley, Patience, and Clarissa lived. The Mulatto designations on Joseph Nichols and his children in post-emancipation census records — and the DNA matches to descendants of white Dutch Fork families — are the documented evidence of that history.

The Migration · Dutch Fork to Texas How the Dutch Fork Community Became the Bellville Community

The Dutch Fork community did not stay in South Carolina. The German immigrant families — and the enslaved people they held — moved westward in the 1820s and 1830s as cotton agriculture expanded into Mississippi and Texas.

The Waldrop family migrated from Newberry County South Carolina through Mississippi to Williamson County and then Austin County Texas — bringing Patience's family with them. The 1839 Rankin County Mississippi deed is the primary source document of that transfer — the last named record of Patience and Clarissa before Texas.

John Nichols — who held Easter Bell and Rendy Blayn in Bellville — fled from Williamson County Tennessee to Austin County Texas in 1835. His wife was Fanny Waldrop — a daughter of the same Waldrop family that held Patience and Clarissa. The Dutch Fork community traveled to Texas through two parallel paths and converged in Bellville.

What we call the Bellville community — the families documented in the 1900 census on the same pages — is the Texas destination of the Dutch Fork migration. The South Carolina upcountry, the Mississippi corridor, the Tennessee flight — all of it ends in Bellville, Austin County, Texas, in the same precinct, on the same land grant, in June 1900 when the census enumerator walked through.

Still Researching What We Are Still Looking For

The Dutch Fork origin of this family is confirmed by the documentary record and the DNA. What remains open:

The exact household where Molley was born — whether in West Africa or in South Carolina. The Sims family who held her in 1793 Union County had roots in the Dutch Fork community. Whether Molley was brought from the coast or born in the upcountry is not yet confirmed.

The European DNA match identities — the 10 ThruLines matches confirming Clarissa and the 46 Waldrop family DNA matches are biological evidence of the Dutch Fork community. Identifying which specific families produced those matches would confirm the exact household where Clarissa's European ancestry originated.

The Francis Matthews Jr. connection — a DNA match at Halifax County Virginia with a neighbor named Jacob Blain — may connect Rendy Blayn's family to the Dutch Fork corridor through Virginia. Contact via GEDmatch has not yet been made: Fanin11@aol.com.
