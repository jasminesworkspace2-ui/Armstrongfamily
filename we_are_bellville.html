Bellville · Austin County · Texas · 1865 to Now

We Are
Bellville.

They stayed. They loved. They multiplied.

They were brought to this land in chains — held by the same handful of men who received colonial land grants from the Mexican government in the 1820s and 1830s. When emancipation came on June 19, 1865, they did not scatter. They stayed on the same land. They found each other. They married each other's children. They built a community out of everything that had been done to them. This is that community. This is our family. We are Bellville.

"Our entire bloodline in Bellville — built off the pain, the brokenness, the slavery. Our families found love, made children and multiplied. We truly are Bellville."

Jasmine M. Lee · Founder, Dock Ellis Foundation · Seven years of research · 2019–2026
The Families · Bellville, Austin County, Texas

These are the families documented in this archive. Some dates are confirmed by primary source. Others come from family trees and oral history still being verified. All of them belong here. This page is a living document — growing as research continues.

Nichols Joseph Nichols × Easter Bell Nichols · Bellville · 1865

The heart of the archive. Joseph born ca. 1847 — born enslaved in the John Nichols household. Easter Bell born July 4, 1838 — born enslaved in the same community. They married December 18, 1865 — six months after Juneteenth. They had six children. Their descendants are the branches of this entire family tree.

Armstrong Dave Armstrong (S.C.) × Clara Morgan (N.C.) · Austin County · 1868

Dave Armstrong was born in South Carolina. Clara Morgan was born in North Carolina. Both came to Austin County Texas after emancipation. They married August 29, 1868. Their children grew up in Bellville — and their son John Armstrong married Hellen Nichols, Easter Bell's daughter, connecting the Armstrong and Nichols families forever.

Pink Hannibal Pink · Bellville · married Easter Bell after Joseph's death

Hannibal Pink was born ca. 1840 in Texas — confirmed in the 1900 Bellville census, married 1865. Easter Bell Nichols married Hannibal Pink after the death of Joseph Nichols. She died in December 1935 as Easter Pink — still in Bellville, still on the same land. The Pink family is confirmed in the 1900 Bellville community. Much of their documentation is still in progress.

Lott Beno Lott (1830–) · William Lott × Rosie Carter · Bellville

The Lott family surname traces to the Lott land grants in Austin County — Arthur Lott and Samuel Lott appear as original grantees in the tax records. The Black Lott family of Bellville descended from people held on those same grants. William Lott married Rosie Carter. Their daughter Lula Lott married Joshua Armstrong — connecting the Lott and Armstrong families.

Nagy · Nazy John Nagy × Clara Nichols · Bellville · married 1892

Clara Nichols — Easter Bell's daughter — married John Nagy on November 5, 1891. Their son Roy Nazy carried the family forward. Roy Nazy had children with Quille McCree — connecting the Nagy and McCree families — and later with Lola Hicklin. The Nazy name is one of the most documented branches of the Easter Bell tree.

McCree Steve McCree × Mollie Chappell · Bellville

Twiggs Louis Twiggs × Ida Armstrong · Bellville

Ida Armstrong — daughter of Dave Armstrong and Clara Morgan, sister of John Armstrong — married Louis Twiggs. They had children in Bellville. Ida died May 19, 1918 in Fort Worth of tuberculosis — six months before her mother Clara Armstrong died. Louis Twiggs is confirmed in the 1900 Bellville census, born March 1870.

Lilly Willie Nichols × Delphia Lilly · Austin County

Willie Nichols — Easter Bell's youngest son — married Delphia Lilly. Their children took the Lilly surname. The family continued in Texas into the 1990s and 2000s. Willie Nichols was named as a legal heir — as Willey Nichols — in the 2006 Austin County tax foreclosure on the John Nichols Survey land.

This Land.
These Names.
This Family.

The John Nichols Survey — Abstract 73 — was a Mexican colonial land grant from 1832. John Nichols held it. He held people on it. When he died in 1864 and emancipation came in 1865, the people he had enslaved inherited fractional interests in that same land. Easter Bell Nichols's name was still on the deed in 2006 — 141 years after emancipation — when Austin County foreclosed for unpaid taxes and the land transferred to the Bellville Independent School District.

They stayed on the land longer than the family that enslaved them. They built more with it. They multiplied from it. Every name on this page — every Nichols, every Armstrong, every Pink, every Lott, every Nazy, every McCree — traces back to that land. To that community. To the people who chose to stay and to love and to build something out of everything that had been taken from them.

We are Bellville.
