Identity control

Individual research file

Fanny is the Waldrop–Hillin–Hutchins–Nichols bridge. The archive preserves Nancy Jane Francis / Frances / “Fanny” Waldrop as one continuous identity across these household and marriage records. This sequence does not prove that Patience, Rachel, Clarissa, Joseph, Easter, Rendy, or any other enslaved person moved with her.

DOCUMENTED HEIR · DOWNSTREAM EVIDENCE ATTACHED ONLY WHEN PERSON-SPECIFIC
Common documentary anchor

1839 · Rankin County

The distribution instrument is the starting point for this dossier. It recites the earlier 1795 gift of Patience to Martha “Patsy” Davenport Waldrop and names Patience's increase, including Clarissa, Rachel, Tom, William, Amanda and Harriet. This heir's exact allotment must be extracted from the instrument rather than inferred.

Primary-source control

Rankin County · Deed Book 4 · p. 435

March 25, 1839. The archive's reconstruction preserves the deed's family relationships as Patience → Rachel → Tom and Patience → Clarissa → William, Amanda, Harriet. The eight Waldrop children belong to the legal distribution layer. The recovered transcription names all eight children together as grantees and conveys Patience and her named descendants to them collectively. This dossier therefore does not assign any one named enslaved person exclusively to this heir without a separate later partition, sale, or other person-specific record.

The archive's established working reconstruction preserves Fanny as one continuous person across James Hillin, the Hutchins household, and John Nichols. ESTABLISHED WORKING IDENTITY · RECORD-BY-RECORD RECONCILIATION
Established chronology

1817 → 1821 → 1827 → 1837

1817 · Maury County, Tennessee: James Hillin phase. 12 July 1821 · Lawrence County, Mississippi: Anthony Hutchins marriage phase. By March 1827 · Texas: Fanny appears in the John Nichols household reconstruction with John Hutchins and Martha Hutchins belonging to the earlier Hutchins phase. 14 October 1837 · Austin County, Texas: legal John Nichols/Fanny marriage. Anthony Hutchins remained alive and later left an Early County, Georgia will dated 1 December 1848 and proved in 1849; what happened between Fanny and Anthony, and the precise 1821–1827 transition, remain unresolved.
