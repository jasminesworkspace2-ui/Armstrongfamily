Fanny / Nancy Jane Waldrop ↗
Mississippi → Texas
Bridge person · status requires record-by-record precision
Established sequence: James Hillin (1817, Maury County, TN) → Anthony Hutchins (12 Jul 1821, Lawrence County, MS) → John/Martha Hutchins → John Nichols household in Texas by Mar 1827 → legal John/Fanny marriage (14 Oct 1837, Austin County, TX). Anthony remained alive; his 1848/1849 Early County, Georgia will means the 1821–1827 transition remains an open research question, not an identity split.
Why she matters: Fanny is the biological and household bridge connecting the Davenport/Waldrop line to the Hutchins and Nichols descendants. She is also one of the eight joint grantees named in the March 25, 1839 Rankin instrument. Neither fact, alone or together, proves that Patience or any particular descendant entered Fanny's individual household or property trail.
Stephen R. Waldrop ↗
1827 direct transfer · 1839 downstream sale · Mississippi → Louisiana
Documented person-specific property trail
1827: Hezekiah transferred Patience, Rachel and Thomas to Stephen. 1839: Stephen is one of the eight joint grantees in the March Rankin instrument.
July 5, 1839 · recorded Aug. 19: Stephen → Howell Prestridge, $850, Rachel with a boy named Briant. The reviewed wording does not clearly state Rachel and Briant's relationship.
Claiborne P. Waldrop ↗

Separate 1847 record: Julia, age 6, is named in Claiborne's documentary trail. No present evidence places Julia in Patience's family.

Documentary Pathway

Davenport → Waldrop → Nichols

This sequence shows what is actually documented, what is a supported reconstruction, and where the missing transfer record still matters.

DOCUMENTEDSUPPORTED CONNECTIONUNRESOLVED
AUG. 4, 1795
Francis Davenport Sr. → Patty Waldrop → Patience

Newberry County deed places Patience in Patty's legal property history. This is the opening anchor for the later Waldrop chain.

JUL. 30, 1827
Ezekiah Waldrop → Stephen R. Waldrop

Hinds County deed names Patience, Rachel, Clara and Tom/Thomas. Clara → Clarissa is treated as a supported identification because the later family cluster aligns.

MAR. 28, 1837
The Waldrop heirs and Patience's “increase”

The family instrument recites the 1795 gift and preserves Patience → Rachel → Tom and Patience → Clarissa → William, Amanda, Harriet. It is a collective heirs instrument; it does not assign each person to a specific heir.

1837 → TEXAS
The missing allocation / movement record

Fanny/Nancy Jane Waldrop is one of Patty's heir generation and is already documented in Texas with John Nicholls by 1827. The unresolved question is which enslaved family members, if any, became part of Fanny's individual interest or household.

PRE-1865
John Nichols household becomes a documented slaveholding site

A tax transcription records John with four enslaved people; the 1860 slave schedule records nine unnamed enslaved people. These records establish slaveholding but do not individually identify Joseph, Henry, Easter, Rendy or another person.

1866–1870
Black Nichols community becomes visible by name

Joe Nichols appears in the 1866 tax index; Joe and Henry Nichols appear on the 1868 Austin County Freedmen's poll-tax list; Henry Nicholas appears in Bellville in 1870 and operates a farm. This is post-emancipation continuity evidence, not a standalone proof of a specific 1860 schedule identity.

Working reconstruction: the combined surname, county, immediate post-emancipation presence, documented Nichols slaveholding, land/community continuity and Fanny's Waldrop inheritance position make the Davenport/Waldrop → Nichols pathway a serious reconstruction to test. The research task is now to identify the mechanism and individuals — not to pretend the networks are unrelated until a single perfect sentence is found.
