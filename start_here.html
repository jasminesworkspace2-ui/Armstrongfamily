Start in Bellville

Welcome to the Bellville story.

Bellville, in Austin County, Texas, is a major setting in the archive. Post-emancipation census, tax, land, probate, and family records help us follow Black families building lives and landownership in the community.

Explore We Are Bellville →Meet the community, the families, and the records that place them in Bellville. Explore The Family →See the family network connected to Bellville.
Start with Easter

Easter Bell Nichols

Easter Bell Nichols was born July 4, 1838, in Texas during slavery and died December 17, 1935, in Bellville. Her post-emancipation life is documented through family, census, death, tax, and land records.

Follow Easter into the next generation →Meet her daughter Hellen Nichols Armstrong and the family that followed. Follow the paper trail →See how records are being used to investigate Easter's earlier life.
Start with DNA

DNA can open a door.

A DNA match can point toward a shared family connection, but the match alone does not establish a specific ancestor or exact relationship. This archive uses DNA alongside census records, vital records, deeds, probate, court records, and other documentary evidence.

Who this archive is for

If your family carries the name Corbin, Ballard, Armstrong, Nichols, Pink, Bell—or another name connected to the archive—or if a DNA test led you here, you may be looking at your own family history.

This archive follows African American family history across South Carolina, Mississippi, Texas, and later generations beyond Texas. It preserves what the records establish while keeping research leads and unanswered questions visible.

You are not expected to know the answer before you begin. That is what this page is for.

How to read the research
Documented

A cited record directly supports the person, event, or relationship being described.

Research Lead

The evidence points toward a possible connection, but the documentary bridge is not complete.

Open Question

The archive does not yet have enough evidence to answer it. The question stays visible so it can be pursued.
