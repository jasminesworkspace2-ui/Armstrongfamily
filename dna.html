DNA Testing · Confirmed Kits

The Tests Behind This Research

What is GEDmatch? GEDmatch is a free third-party DNA analysis platform where people upload their DNA test results from AncestryDNA, 23andMe, FTDNA, or other testing companies. It allows researchers to compare DNA across different testing platforms — so an AncestryDNA tester and a 23andMe tester can find each other as matches. GEDmatch also offers specialized ancestry calculators like the EthioHelix Africa-Only analysis used in this research. Kit numbers on GEDmatch are assigned when DNA is uploaded and serve as the identifier for each person's DNA file.

Privacy note: In accordance with GEDmatch Terms of Service, specific kit numbers and email addresses of other researchers are not published publicly on this page. Match names and cM values are shared only where the researcher has made their profile public or has been contacted directly. All outreach follows GEDmatch messaging guidelines.

The family's African ancestry draws from three distinct source populations that were brought together through the Atlantic slave trade:

Primary — Sahelian West Africa: The Fulani single-population match combined with the consistent North Africa component at 18.16% points to ancestry from the West African Sahel — the belt running from Senegambia through Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger, northern Nigeria, and Cameroon. These populations absorbed North African ancestry through trans-Saharan trade over centuries before enslavement.

Secondary — Forest West Africa / Nigerian: Yoruba, Igbo, Bamoun, Kongo appearing consistently across all approximations points to forest belt West and Central African ancestry — Nigeria, Cameroon, and the Congo basin. These are the populations most heavily represented in the slave trade routes through the Bight of Benin and Bight of Biafra.

Tertiary — East and Central Africa: Hema from the Great Lakes region appearing in virtually every 4-population result, plus East Africa 2 at 12.17%, indicates a Central/East African thread through Bantu-speaking populations that expanded from Central Africa westward.

These results represent the maternal line only — tracing back through Hjordis, Margie, Eva, Hellen, Easter Bell, Joseph Nichols, and ultimately to Patience — born ca. 1765 in South Carolina. The Fulani/Sahelian West African signal is consistent with Upper Guinea and Senegambian slave trade routes through the port of Charleston, South Carolina in the mid-to-late 1700s when Patience was brought to the Americas.

Forensic Corrections · Database Indexing Errors · August 2026

Campbell Family · Database Errors Corrected

The 1870 Austin County Texas census household of John Campbell Sr. was misindexed as White in major genealogy databases. Examination of the original handwritten ledger completed by Assistant Marshal Max Meissner confirms the family was designated M (Mulatto) and B (Black) in the Color column. This error caused standard database searches to return no results for this family.

Error 1 — Racial Misclassification

John Campbell Sr., Susan Campbell, and children William, Moses, Edwin, John Jr., and James were all indexed as White. The original handwritten census sheet designates them as Mulatto and Black. Always search with Race filter blank or unchecked when searching for this family.

Error 2 — Hannah Campbell Inferred Mother Trap

Database algorithms designated Hannah Campbell (age 45, born Mississippi) as the inferred mother of Moses Campbell (age 11). The original sheet shows Hannah was a Domestic Servant in the household — not Moses's mother. Moses and his siblings were born in Texas to North Carolina-born parents John and Susan Campbell.

Error 3 — 1880 Alabama Birthplace Discrepancy

Moses Campbell in 1880 was a 21-year-old illiterate farm servant working for German immigrant Jacob Rinn. His parents' birthplaces were listed as Alabama — contradicting the 1870 North Carolina designation. The 1870 record where John and Susan Campbell gave their own information is the primary baseline. The 1880 Alabama entry reflects an estimate by Moses or his employer.

Two Eliminated Duplicate Families

Brazoria County — ELIMINATED: Mose C. Campbell married Laura Edwards 1901 — parents born Kentucky and Texas — confirmed separate coastal family. San Antonio 1919 — ELIMINATED: Death certificate #23051 for Mrs. M. Campbell — confirmed female housewife — informant Charlie Applewhite — not the male Moses Campbell.
