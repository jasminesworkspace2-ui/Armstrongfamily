THE TEXAS BRANCH — Fanny, John Nichols, Rendy, Easter, Joseph

Person Where, When
Fanny Waldrop (Ezekiah's daughter) SC → TN (married 1817) → MS (married 1821) → Austin County, TEXAS by 1837
John Nichols Austin County, Texas from 1827/1834 onward. Died there March 1864.
Rendy Blayn Only ever documented in Bellville, Texas. Birthplace before Texas unresolved.
Easter Bell Only ever documented in Bellville, Texas. Born there 1838.
Joseph Nichols Only ever documented in Bellville, Texas. Born there ~1847.

None of the Texas people (Rendy, Easter, Joseph, John Nichols) were ever in South Carolina, Georgia, Tennessee, or Mississippi. If a record shows any of these four in one of those states, it is a different person.

Fanny is the ONLY confirmed bridge between the Mississippi family and the Texas family — and she moved BEFORE the 1839 deed, not after.

THE ONE UNPROVEN LINK — What Would Actually Connect the Two Branches

No document has ever shown Patience, Clarissa, Rachel, William, Amanda, or Harriet physically traveling to Texas.

The only real, still-open lead that could connect them: Claiborne Waldrop bought out his siblings' shares of Ezekiah's estate in Sabine Parish, Louisiana, around 1847. If that document exists and names people who were later moved to Texas, that would be the bridge. Nothing else currently qualifies.

FAST FILTER — Before You Paste a New Record, Ask:

  1. What state is it in?
  2. South Carolina, Georgia, Tennessee before 1815 → could be Patience/Clarissa/Rachel's era
  3. Mississippi 1815–1839 → could be Patience/Clarissa/Rachel
  4. Louisiana before 1839 → cannot be Patience, Clarissa, or Rachel (they weren't there yet)
  5. Louisiana 1839 onward → could be Clarissa or Rachel

  6. Texas, any date → can only be Fanny, John Nichols, Rendy, Easter, or Joseph — never Patience, Clarissa, or Rachel

  7. What year is it?

  8. Compare against the tables above. If the person's confirmed location that year doesn't match the record's location, it's probably a different person with the same name.

  9. Is the name common? Patience, Easter, Clarissa, Fanny, Amanda, Rachel, and Harriet all recur independently, dozens of times, across unconnected families in this exact region and era. A name match alone is never enough — it needs a matching place AND year too.
