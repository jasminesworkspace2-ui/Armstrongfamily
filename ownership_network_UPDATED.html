THE OWNERSHIP NETWORK

The legal records that intersected the lives of the people I am trying to find.

Before a family tree can show relationships, the records often force us to confront the system around them. This map follows the deeds, estates, purchases, gifts, and transfers that placed enslaved people inside the legal property networks of South Carolina and Mississippi. It does not tell the whole story. It tells us what the surviving records say.

Honest Limits

What This Map Cannot Tell Us

It cannot show every person who was enslaved.

It cannot show every family relationship.

It cannot show every separation, sale, birth, death, resistance, or act of survival.

It cannot prove parentage from a surname, age, geography, DNA match, or similar name alone.

It cannot assume that every person named in a probate document was owned by the person who signed as appraiser, witness, administrator, or executor.

It cannot turn an unresolved lead into a fact just because the visualization looks convincing.

The records are not the people. They are traces left by a legal system that recorded people as property. But sometimes, inside those records, a relationship survives: a mother, a child, a son, a family group. This page follows those traces carefully.

I am not building this map to make the past look complete.
I am building it so I can see where the evidence ends — and where the search begins.
