For Family Members · Plain Language

This page puts our family's story in order by date — and shows what was happening in America at the same time. If you want to understand why Easter Bell's life was so remarkable, seeing what she lived through on a timeline makes it real. Every date in the family column is confirmed by a primary source document.

Seeing what was happening in America at the same moment places our ancestors' choices in context.

Year American History Our Family
Enslavementca. 1765 – 1832
ca. 1765 American colonies still under British rule. Slavery entrenched across the South. Cotton has not yet transformed the economy — tobacco and rice dominate. Molley — Patience's mother — is now the oldest confirmed ancestor in this family. She was enslaved by James Sims Sr. of Union County South Carolina. In 1793 she was listed by name in a mortgage document alongside her seven children: Charles, Edward, Delf, Abram, Sawny, Tom, and Patience. She is Jasmine M. Lee's 6th great-grandmother. Newberry County SC Deed Book B Page 467 · March 22 1793 Patience born — South Carolina — Molley's daughter — our 5th great-grandmother. Her life before 1793 is not documented in any record found to date.Deed Book B Page 467 · 1793 · Deed Book C Pages 367-368 · August 4 1795
1776 Declaration of Independence. "All men are created equal." Approximately 500,000 enslaved people in the colonies are not included in that declaration. Patience is approximately 11 years old. She is enslaved in South Carolina.
1793 Eli Whitney invents the cotton gin. Cotton becomes enormously profitable overnight. Demand for enslaved labor surges across the South. The domestic slave trade expands rapidly. Patience is approximately 28 years old — in Newberry County South Carolina in the Waldrop-Davenport household.
Aug 4, 1795 The United States signs the Treaty of Greenville opening the Northwest Territory. The domestic slave trade is expanding. South Carolina is a major hub. Patience transferred to Patty Waldrup — Francis Davenport gives Patience to Patty Waldrup — Newberry County South Carolina — August 4 1795. The first primary source document confirming she existed.Newberry County SC Deed Book C Page 367 · August 4 1795
ca. 1807 The United States officially bans the international slave trade in 1808. The domestic trade — buying and selling people already in the country — continues and grows. Prices for enslaved people rise. Clarissa born — South Carolina — Patience's daughter. Later named in an 1839 Rankin County Mississippi deed confirming the family's migration from South Carolina to Mississippi.1839 Rankin County Mississippi deed · Clarissa named
Antebellum1820 – 1860
1820 The Missouri Compromise. The forced migration of enslaved people from the Upper South to the Deep South — the Second Middle Passage — is at its peak. Over one million people will be relocated by 1860. Patience and Clarissa are in the Waldrop household. Their exact location in 1820 is not confirmed — somewhere between South Carolina and Mississippi in this era.
1827 Mexico opens Texas to American colonization under Stephen F. Austin. Slavery is technically illegal under Mexican law, but the colonists bring it anyway and the Mexican government does not enforce the ban. John Nichols arrives in Texas — registered in Austin's Family Register — bringing enslaved people with him. He will receive a Spanish land grant of one league — 4,428 acres — in Austin County in 1832. That land becomes Bellville.Austin's Family Register 1827 · Spanish land grant July 21 1832
Oct 14, 1837 Texas is an independent republic. Slavery is legal and growing rapidly in Texas. Stephen F. Austin has died. The Republic of Texas is seeking annexation by the United States. John Nichols marries Fanny Waldrup — October 14 1837 — Austin County Texas — Volume A Page 27. The Waldrop family held Patience in South Carolina in 1795. The same family — on the same land — two generations later.Austin County Marriage Records Volume A Page 27 · October 14 1837
Jul 4, 1838 Texas is an independent republic seeking annexation by the United States. Slavery is expanding rapidly. The Cherokee Nation is being forcibly removed from their lands across the South. Easter Bell born — July 4 1838 — Bellville, Austin County Texas. Born enslaved on the John Nichols plantation. Her mother is Rendy Blayn. She is born on Independence Day.Texas death certificate State File No. 54496 · born July 4 1838
Dec 10, 1840 Texas is still an independent republic — it will be annexed by the United States in 1845. The forced migration of enslaved people westward is accelerating with the expansion of cotton cultivation. Lewis F. Ballard Sr. purchases Mississippi land — 196.6 acres — Washington County Mississippi — Mt. Salus Land Office. Newton Ballard is approximately age 10 — enslaved in this household in Mississippi before being brought to Texas.Mississippi Land Patents Documents 23028 and 23029 · December 10 1840
Feb 1845 Texas is annexed by the United States on December 29 1845 — entering as a slave state. The Mexican-American War follows shortly after as the US expands westward. Joseph Nichols born — February 1845 — Bellville, Austin County Texas. Born enslaved on the same plantation as Easter Bell. They are seven years apart. They will marry in December 1869.1900 Federal Census · Joseph Nichols · birth February 1845
1850 The Fugitive Slave Act. Enslaved people who escape to free states can be captured and returned. The country is moving toward crisis. There are now 3.2 million enslaved people in the United States. Newton Ballard enslaved in Smith County Texas — unnamed male age approximately 20 born Mississippi — Page 458 of the Smith County slave schedule under Lewis F. Ballard Sr. age 58 born Florida.United States Federal Census Slave Schedule 1850 · Smith County Texas · Page 458
Aug 26, 1856 Bleeding Kansas — pro-slavery and anti-slavery settlers are fighting in Kansas Territory. The Republican Party has just nominated its first presidential candidate. The country is fracturing. Newton Ballard files for land — August 26 1856 — 160 acres — Upshur County Texas — Texas GLO File No. 3378. His enslaver Lewis F. Ballard Sr. is still alive. Newton files the claim anyway.Texas GLO File No. 3378 · Survey No. 124 · Abstract 131 · August 26 1856
Feb 18, 1857 The Dred Scott decision — March 1857 — rules that Black people are not citizens. The Supreme Court has spoken. The country is six months from Lincoln's election and four years from war. Lewis F. Ballard Sr. dies — February 18 1857 — six months after Newton's land claim. Newton Ballard outlives him. The 160-acre claim remains.New Hope Missionary Baptist Church history · Smith County Texas
1860 Abraham Lincoln elected president. South Carolina secedes in December. The Civil War is months away. There are 4 million enslaved people in the United States. Easter Bell age 22 and Rendy Blayn age 43 confirmed in John Nichols' 1860 slave schedule in Austin County Texas — listed only as unnamed females by age. Easter is twenty-two years old and already has children.1860 Federal Census Slave Schedule · Austin County Texas · John Nichols
Civil War1861 – 1865
Sep 4, 1861 Civil War is five months old. Confederate forces have won the First Battle of Bull Run. Texas has seceded. Confederate Texas is drafting white men for the war effort. Rev. George W. Ballard born — September 4 1861 — Newton Ballard's son — Smith or Upshur County Texas. Born during the Civil War. He will become a minister and live in Waco.McLennan County Death Certificate Volume 4 Page 483
Mar 4, 1864 The Civil War enters its final year. Ulysses S. Grant takes command of Union forces. Sherman is planning his March to the Sea. The Confederacy is losing. John Nichols dies — March 4 1864 — Austin County Texas — confirmed by two sworn eyewitness testimonies in 1876. His estate lists ten enslaved people unnamed. Easter Bell and Rendy Blayn are among them.1876 pension affidavit · S.S. Reams and Nathaniel Reed · Bellville courthouse
Jun 19, 1865 Juneteenth — Union soldiers arrive in Galveston Texas and announce emancipation. Texas is the last Confederate state to receive the news. The Civil War has been over for two months. Four million people are free. Easter Bell and Rendy Blayn are legally free. Easter is twenty-six years old. Rendy is approximately forty-seven. They stay in Bellville — on the same land where they were held. They build their free lives where their enslaved lives were.Juneteenth · June 19 1865 · Galveston Texas
Reconstruction1865 – 1877
Jan 27, 1868 The Reconstruction Acts of 1867 — Congress requires Southern states to allow Black men to vote. Texas is under military rule. Black Texans are registering to vote across the state. Joseph Nichols registers to vote — January 27 1868 — Bellville — Line No. 1802. He cannot read. He cannot write. He registers anyway.Texas Voter Records 1867-1918 · Line No. 1802
Dec 23, 1869 Reconstruction is in full force. Black elected officials hold office across Texas. Schools for freedpeople are being built. The brief window of Black political power is open. Joseph Nichols and Easter Bell marry — December 23 1869 — Bellville courthouse — Marriage License No. 8 — officiated by Rev. J. Shirl. One week earlier Green Nichols married Eliza — License No. 7. The community is legalizing what already existed.Austin County Marriage Records Volume A · License No. 8 · December 23 1869
Jun 1870 The 1870 census — the first to list formerly enslaved people by name — documents the Bellville Black community. The Ku Klux Klan is terrorizing Black communities across the South. Amanda Brown confirmed in Bellville — recorded as Manda Reese in Household 114 — age 40 — born Georgia — already in Texas after crossing the South alone from Wilcox County Georgia. Capital M confirmed on original microfilm.1870 Federal Census · Beat No. 1 · Austin County Texas · Page 18 · direct image examination
1870 Black landowners are establishing themselves across the South. White supremacist violence is simultaneously trying to destroy what freedpeople are building. The tension between these two forces defines Reconstruction. Green Nichols holds 30 acres on Abstract 73 — the John Nichols Survey — the same land where he was enslaved. Joseph holds 2 acres. Alfred and Thomas hold fractional lots. Four formerly enslaved men hold land on the plantation where they were born.Austin County Texas Tax Assessment Roll · Abstract 73 · J. Nichols Survey
Sep 1870 Reconstruction governments are building schools and courts. Black elected officials hold office in Texas. The Fifteenth Amendment — ratified in 1870 — has guaranteed Black men the right to vote. Hellen Nichols born — September 1870 — Bellville Texas — Joseph and Easter Bell's daughter. She was not in the June 1870 census because she had not yet been born. She will be Jasmine's great-great-grandmother.1900 Federal Census · Hellen Nichols birth September 1870 · 2006 Austin County tax suit
Jim Crow1877 – 1915
1877 The Compromise of 1877 ends Reconstruction. Federal troops withdraw from the South. Jim Crow laws begin rolling back everything gained during Reconstruction. The brief period of Black political power is over. Easter Bell is thirty-eight years old. Joseph is thirty-two. The Nichols freedmen community continues farming on Abstract 73. Their children — Allen, Oscar, Josephine, Clara, Hellen — are growing up free in Bellville.
Aug 26, 1897 The same calendar date — forty-one years earlier — Newton Ballard filed his freedom land claim on August 26 1856. Jim Crow is fully established across the South. Lynching is at its peak. James Wilson Ballard Sr. born — August 26 1897 — Palestine, Anderson County Texas — son of Rev. George W. Ballard and Mary Ann Edwards Ballard. Same calendar date as Newton Ballard's land claim forty-one years before.Texas death certificate · James Wilson Ballard Sr. · born August 26 1897
Jun 27, 1900 The nadir of American race relations. Between 1890 and 1910 more than 1,100 Black Americans are lynched. Texas has one of the highest lynching rates in the South. Plessy v. Ferguson has made segregation the law of the land. Eva Armstrong born — June 27 1900 — Bellville, Austin County Texas — daughter of John Armstrong and Hellen Nichols Armstrong — granddaughter of Easter Bell Nichols. She will carry this family to California.1900 Federal Census · Bellville Austin County Texas · Eva Armstrong age 4/12 born June 1900
ca. 1905 W.E.B. Du Bois founds the Niagara Movement — precursor to the NAACP. The organized struggle for Black civil rights is beginning to take structured form. Joseph Nichols dies — ca. 1905 — buried at Jeff Cemetery, Bellville. Easter Bell is sixty-six years old. She will live thirty more years — through Jim Crow, the Great Migration, the Depression, and into the New Deal era.Jeff Cemetery · Bellville Texas
Aug 30, 1911 Racial violence is endemic in Texas. In 1916 Jesse Washington will be burned alive in Waco before a crowd of 10,000. The legal system consistently fails to hold white perpetrators accountable. John Armstrong shot and killed — Fort Worth — by Jack Thompson — Texas State Death Certificate No. 18682. Eva Armstrong is eleven years old. Thompson is charged. Charges dismissed for lack of witnesses. He walks free.Texas State Death Certificate No. 18682 · John Armstrong · August 30 1911
Great Migration1915 – 1950
1915 The Great Migration begins. Hundreds of thousands of Black Americans leave the South for northern and western cities — Chicago, Detroit, Los Angeles, Oakland — fleeing Jim Crow and looking for something better. Easter Bell Nichols is seventy-six years old. She stays in Bellville. Her daughter Hellen is in Fort Worth. Her granddaughter Eva is in Fort Worth. The family is beginning to spread — but Easter Bell stays on the land.
Mar 13, 1929 The stock market crashes in October 1929 — the Great Depression begins. Black Americans, last hired and first fired, are hit hardest. Unemployment in Black communities will reach 50% or more. Margie Nell Ballard born — March 13 1929 — Fort Worth Texas — 306½ East Annie Street — daughter of James Wilson Ballard Sr. and Eva Armstrong Ballard. File No. 26658. Born at the start of the Depression.Texas Birth Certificate File No. 26658 · March 13 1929
Dec 17, 1935 The New Deal is providing relief but largely excludes Black workers — domestic workers and agricultural laborers are intentionally left out of Social Security and labor protections at the insistence of Southern Democrats. Easter Bell Nichols dies — December 17 1935 — Bellville, Austin County Texas — age 97. Born enslaved July 4 1838. Died free. She outlived her enslaver by seventy-one years. Margie Nell is six years old.Texas death certificate State File No. 54496 · Easter Bell Nichols · December 17 1935
Mar 16, 1939 The Depression is grinding on. World War II is six months away. Black families in the South are among the most economically devastated communities in the country. Thelma Louise Ballard dies — San Antonio — age three months — cause: malnutrition and dehydration — Certificate No. 11120. Margie is nine years old. Her infant sister is buried at EastView Cemetery San Antonio.Texas death certificate No. 11120 · Thelma Louise Ballard · March 16 1939
Mar 13, 1953 The Korean War has just ended. Thurgood Marshall is arguing Brown v. Board of Education before the Supreme Court. The Civil Rights Movement is building momentum. James Wilson Ballard Sr. dies — March 13 1953 — Austin State Hospital — Travis County Texas — on Margie Nell's twenty-fourth birthday. Cause: bilateral tuberculosis and psychosis with syphilitic meningoencephalitis.Texas death certificate · James Wilson Ballard Sr. · March 13 1953
Civil Rights1954 – 1975
1955 Rosa Parks refuses to give up her seat — December 1 1955. Emmett Till murdered in Mississippi. The Montgomery Bus Boycott begins. The Civil Rights Movement is making itself unmistakably known. Eva Armstrong Ballard testifies — San Antonio — subpoenaed in a police brutality case — she appears and testifies. Her father was killed by a white man who walked free. She is fifty-four years old. She testifies anyway.San Antonio Texas newspaper records · 1955
Jan 20, 1960 The Civil Rights Movement is at its height. The Greensboro sit-ins begin in February 1960. The Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee is formed. The Civil Rights Act is four years away. Hjordis L. Corbin born — January 20 1960 — daughter of Margie Nell Ballard Corbin and Norman Caswell Corbin — AncestryDNA kit SQ8499767 — 3,575.4 cM shared with Jasmine M. Lee confirming parent-child relationship.AncestryDNA kit SQ8499767 · Hjordis L. Corbin
Apr 21, 1974 The Civil Rights Act and Voting Rights Act are law. Nixon has resigned. The long work of implementation continues against fierce resistance across the South and nationwide. Norman Caswell Corbin dies — April 21 1974 — Riverview Hospital — Santa Ana California — age 53 — pneumonia. Publisher of the Orange County Star Review. NAACP vice president. 1,500 mourners at his funeral.Orange County California newspaper obituary · Norman C. Corbin · April 21 1974
Nov 24, 1977 Jimmy Carter is president. The Great Migration has reshaped cities. The Civil Rights era has transformed American law — if not fully American reality. Eva Armstrong Ballard dies — November 24 1977 — Garden Grove California — age 77. Granddaughter of Easter Bell Nichols. Born in Bellville Texas. Died in California. She crossed the entire distance between those two worlds.Family oral history · confirmed by Hjordis Corbin · Sonsery Corbin
