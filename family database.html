Blacks Found in the Deeds of Laurens & Newberry Counties SC · 1785-1827 South Carolina Primary Source Deeds

These are primary source documents from the deed books of Laurens County and Newberry County, South Carolina — abstracted by Margaret Peckham Motes (Genealogical Publishing Co., Baltimore, 2002). Each document names enslaved people and the white families who held them. The Nichols, Waldrop, and Davenport families all appear as witnesses, creditors, and enslavers in this same deed book community between 1785 and 1827.

Deed Book C: 294-295 July 23, 1792 — Recorded June 3, 1795 — Newberry County SC Bill of Sale — Daniel Williams Sr. to John Williams and John Williams Jr.

27 enslaved people named: Phil · Jack · Will · Kyzer · Moses · Harry · Jacob · Jude · Isbell · Ruth · Easter (girl about 14 years old) · Rose · Aggy · Aron · John · Dave · Frankey · Suckey · Sarah · Edom · Little Fill · Solomon · George · James · Lett · Winney · Harris. Consideration: £1,500 Virginia Currency.

Easter age 14 in July 1792 — the same Easter who appears as age 17 in Deed Book F: 23 (May 30, 1795) — tracked across two documents three years apart. Julius Nichols Jr. J.P. proves the companion Secret Trust document. The name Easter documented in the Newberry County community 46 years before Easter Bell Nichols was born in Texas.
Deed Book C: 296 July 23, 1792 / Proved April 22, 1793 — Recorded June 8, 1795 — Newberry County SC Secret Trust Declaration — John Williams Sr. for Daniel Williams Sr.

John Williams Sr. declares he holds all 27 enslaved people (same as C: 294-295) "subject to the control direction and order and command of the said Daniel Williams who is the real and true owner." Signed April 20, 1793. Witnesses: Charles Colcock, Stephen Heard.

Proved by Julius Nichols Jr., J.P. — April 22, 1793. CRITICAL: The Nichols family is confirmed legally active in Laurens/Newberry County South Carolina in 1793 — the same deed book series and the same community as Patience, Molley, and the Waldrop-Davenport network.
Deed Book C — Debt Sale (ca. January 1795) Bond dated January 1, 1795 — Newberry County SC Debt Sale — Unnamed seller to Wadsworth and Turpin, Merchants

Seven enslaved people: Moll (about 30 years old) · Patience (about 30 years old) · Hannah (about 12) · Lin (about 11) · Violet (9) · Esther (7) · George (3). Sold in open market to secure payment of £87 12 shillings 2p.

FOUNDATIONAL. Moll and Patience — the two confirmed founding ancestors of this archive — appear together in this debt sale. Wadsworth and Turpin acquire them. Lin (age 11) is later confirmed freed by Thomas Wadsworth's will (Deed Book E: 624-625), proving Wadsworth acquired this entire group. Moll and Patience were NOT freed by Wadsworth's will — they were transferred elsewhere between January and August 1795.
Deed Book C: 259 March 28, 1795 — Recorded May 29, 1795 — Newberry County SC Mortgage — Daniel Williams to James Waldrop and Samuel Henderson

Enslaved people mortgaged as collateral: Harry · Moses · Jude and her children George and Lena · Sarah. Payment of £400 Sterling.

James Waldrop confirmed as creditor of Daniel Williams. The same Daniel Williams who held Easter and the 27-person community. The Waldrop family is acquiring financial control over the Williams enslaved community — five months before Patty Waldrop receives Patience from Francis Davenport in August 1795.
Deed Book C: 259 May 8, 1795 — Proved May 18, 1795 — Recorded June 5, 1795 — Newberry County SC Bill of Sale — Francis Davenport Sr. to Francis Davenport Jr.

One enslaved man: Anthony (about 18 years old). Price: £20 Sterling. Witness: Isaac Davenport. Proved by Isaac Davenport before Charles Griffin J.P.

The same Francis Davenport Sr. who gives Patience to Patty Waldrop three months later. The same Charles Griffin J.P. who will certify the August 4 Patience deed. The Davenport family is actively managing enslaved people in the months surrounding the foundational Patience document.
Deed Book C: 367-368 August 4, 1795 — Newberry County SC Deed of Gift — Francis Davenport Sr. to his daughter Patty Waldrop

Patience — and her future increase. Proved by Charles Griffin J.P.

THE FOUNDATIONAL DOCUMENT OF THIS ARCHIVE. Patience given to Patty Waldrop. This is the primary source that establishes Patience in the Waldrop family line that leads through Clarissa → Rendy Blayn → Easter Bell Nichols → Hellen → Eva → Margie → Hjordis → Jasmine M. Lee.
Deed Book D: 280 January 2, 1799 — Recorded July 28, 1799 — Laurens/Newberry County SC Deed of Gift — Spencer Morgan of Orangeburgh District to his daughter Fanney Morgan

Five enslaved people: Pompey (man) · Dinah (woman) · Littye · Newman · Mary (Dinah's three children).

John Nichols witnesses this deed. Confirmed in the same Laurens/Newberry County deed book community in 1799 — four years after the Patience deed and six years after Julius Nichols Jr. J.P. appeared in the same books. The Nichols family is present and legally active in this community throughout the foundational period of this archive.
Deed Books E: 613-629 and F: 1-5 May 10, 1802 — Proved 1802-1803 — Newberry County SC Deeds of Emancipation — Thomas Wadsworth's Will (executors: William Turpin and Benjamin Cudworth)

Freed by will: Cate (~18) · Archibald (mulatto, ~9) · Jenny (~37) · Charlott (~3) · Jen (~28) · Thomas (mulatto, ~10) · Line (~11) · Sarah Threet (~35) · Letty (~60) · Mike (~24) · Tilda (~5) · Chloe (~6) · Lize (~14) · Phillis (~6) · Julias (boy, ~4) · Betty (~45) · Fanny (born December 28, 1801 — daughter of Black Sarah, a free woman)

Line freed = Lin age 11 from the January 1795 debt sale. This confirms Wadsworth and Turpin acquired the entire January 1795 group including Moll and Patience. Moll and Patience were NOT freed by Wadsworth's will — they were transferred elsewhere before his 1799 death. The critical undiscovered document: who received Patience from Wadsworth between January and August 1795?
Deed Book F: 23 May 30, 1795 — Proved June 2-3, 1795 before Thomas Wadsworth — Recorded July 21, 1795 Bill of Sale — Daniel Williams Sr. to Elizabeth Williams and Ursla Williams

15 enslaved people — £434 Sterling: Jack (~52) · Jacob (~21) · Rose (~42) · Latt (~14) · Saxon (~11) · John · James (~6) · Winney (~5) · Virgin (~3 months) · Easter (woman about 17 years of age) · Ben (~1) · Sukky (~15) · Aggy (~45) · Phill (~6) · Isham (~1).

Easter age 17 in May 1795 = Easter age 14 in July 1792 (Deed Book C: 294-295) — confirmed same person across two documents. Thomas Wadsworth proves this deed — the same merchant whose firm held Patience in January 1795. Easter and Patience are being transferred in the same community, by the same legal officials, in the same three-month period of 1795.
Deed Book C: 147 February 7, 1795 — Recorded March 31, 1795 — Newberry County SC Bill of Sale — John Barlow to Andrew Smith Jr. and Elizabeth Ann Smith

12 enslaved people including Susanah and her children: Fortune · Santa Croix (~5 years old) · Hariot · Ireland.

Santa Croix — a child named after Saint Croix island — documented in the Newberry County enslaved community in February 1795. The same community as Patience and Molley. Whether this reflects Caribbean family connections in the Newberry County community is an open observation — not connected to the Ester Nicholas hypothesis.
Deed Book F: 53 March 28, 1795 — Newberry County SC Bill of Sale — Daniel Williams Sr. to Samuel Henderson and James Waldrop

One enslaved man: Harry. Part of the £400 debt to Henderson and Waldrop (companion to the mortgage at Deed Book C: 259).

James Waldrop confirmed as a direct buyer of enslaved people from Daniel Williams in 1795 — the same Daniel Williams whose community included Easter, Moll, and Patience. The Waldrop family's financial entanglement with the Williams enslaved community is documented across multiple deed book entries.

Open Research Thread · St. Croix, Danish West Indies · 1819-1826 Ester Nicholas · Morning Star Plantation

This tab summarizes the confirmed primary sources for Ester Nicholas of St. Croix and the DNA evidence pointing toward a connection with this family. For the full research page see ester_nicholas.html. The connection to Easter Bell Nichols of Texas is documented as a hypothesis — not confirmed. The primary sources for Ester Nicholas herself are confirmed.

Three names — three different people. Esther Sam / Ester Nicholas (St. Croix, freed 1819) · Easter Bell Nichols (Bellville TX, born 1838) · The Louisiana Nicholas families (Ascension and Saint James Parishes, DNA match to Hjordis). These are three separate research threads that may or may not connect.

National Archives M1883, Roll 2 — Record Group 55 April 1, 1819 — Free Brief January 14, 1820 Freedom Charter — Esther Sam / Ester Nicholas — Morning Star Plantation, St. Croix

Line 37 — Esther Sam — Negro/Black — Morning Star Plantation — Administrator: H.C. Francis — Freed by A.C. Fibiger April 1, 1819 — Read in court June 29, 1819 — Free Brief issued January 14, 1820 — Recorded as Ester Nicholas — Record No. 1395. Grouped with Sarah M. v. Berger (Mulatto, Line 35), Rebecca C. Canter (Mustee, Line 36), Juliana Wilhelm (Mulatto, Line 38).

CONFIRMED PRIMARY SOURCE. Her name in bondage was Esther Sam. Her name in freedom was Ester Nicholas. She was the only one of the four women classified as Negro — fully African ancestry. She received her freedom at approximately age 38 (born ca. 1781 on Paulus Plantation).
Denmark, Free Colored Censuses 1815-1832 — Record No. 1395 1820 Free Colored Census — Ester Nicholas — St. Croix, Danish West Indies

Ester Nicholas — Female — Negro — Free — Occupation: Domestic (Husarbejde in Danish) — Freed by A.C. Fibiger 1819 — Not registered in 1816 census (was enslaved then). Others in record: John · Charles · Latticia · Louise · Anna Maria Rosina · Salomon · Baltazar · Maria · Peggy Thompson · Penny · Andrew Gordon · Madlane · Cecilia · Maria Wright · Joseph Salomon · Nancy · Johan Peter Recenke · Betsy · Less

CONFIRMED PRIMARY SOURCE. Confirms her freedom status, occupation, and community. The name Joseph Salomon in the household group may connect to the "Sam" identifier in her bondage name — Esther Sam potentially meaning Esther associated with Salomon/Sam.
Danish West Indies Records RG 55 — National Archives July 1, 1819 — Christiansted, St. Croix Pantebog/Skøde — Formal Deed — Names Nicholas and Pheabe as Ancestral Couple

Key passage (Danish): "...navnlig Nicholas og Pheabe og dernes Afkom..." — namely Nicholas and Pheabe and their descendants. Central figure: Susanna Smith (free woman of color). Named heirs: Robert, Paul, Margareth, Isabella Grant. Legal principal: Ann Mary Robertson. Curator: William Purcell. Witnesses: H.C. Francis and E.H. Colbiörnsen.

FOUNDATIONAL. Nicholas is a PERSON — a man — paired with a woman named Pheabe. Their descendants are legally registered. Ester Sam took the surname Nicholas because she descended from this Nicholas. H.C. Francis witnesses this registration three months after Ester's freedom — the same Morning Star administrator.
Register of Black Communicants — St. Croix April 23, 1826 — Morning Star Estate — Administrator: Mad. Seold Holy Communion Record — Ester Sam · Line 177 · Morning Star · Free

Line 177: Ester Sam (also known as Ester Nicholas) — Negro — Free — taking Holy Communion at Morning Star estate. Same page: Catharine Deisler (Mulatto) · Julia M de Francis (Mulatto) · Sophia F de Francis (Samboe) · Thomas Petersen (Negro) · Anna M M Morgen (Mustee).

LAST CONFIRMED PRIMARY SOURCE FOR ESTER NICHOLAS. She is alive, free, and part of the Morning Star community seven years after her freedom. The de Francis names (Julia and Sophia) connect to H.C. Francis. After April 1826 no further primary source has been found in American digital records.
