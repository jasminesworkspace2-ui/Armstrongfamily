Common documentary anchor

1839 · Rankin County

The distribution instrument is the starting point for this dossier. It recites the earlier 1795 gift of Patience to Martha “Patsy” Davenport Waldrop and names Patience's increase, including Clarissa, Rachel, Tom, William, Amanda and Harriet. This heir's exact allotment must be extracted from the instrument rather than inferred.

Primary-source control

Rankin County · Deed Book 4 · p. 435

March 25, 1839. The archive's reconstruction preserves the deed's family relationships as Patience → Rachel → Tom and Patience → Clarissa → William, Amanda, Harriet. The eight Waldrop children belong to the legal distribution layer. Until the original deed is transcribed allocation by allocation, this dossier will not assign any one of those named enslaved people to this heir merely because the heir is a recipient.

Documented fact comes directly from a record. A supported connection can link records without proving custody. A research lead identifies something to test. Unresolved means the archive preserves the gap rather than silently assigning an enslaved person to this heir.

Person-specific evidence

Estate interests and Julia

The network research records Claiborne purchasing heirs' estate interests. A separate 1847 purchase names Julia, age 6. Julia is therefore a named person in Claiborne's documentary trail. This record does not prove that Julia was Clarissa's child, and anonymous slave-schedule entries must not be used to manufacture that connection.

Louisiana evidence

1860–1861 · Sabine Parish

The archive carries a lead to the 1860 U.S. slave schedule for C. P. Waldrop in Sabine Parish. A separate 1912 parish history lists “C. P. Waldrup” among residents who owned six or more enslaved people in 1861. The history is secondary evidence; the schedule image and Louisiana probate/land records need image-level review before unnamed schedule entries are assigned identities.
