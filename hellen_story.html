She was the first person in her direct line born free. Her mother Easter Bell had been enslaved for twenty-seven years. Her father Joseph Nichols had been enslaved for twenty. Hellen was born five years after Juneteenth. She never knew — and neither did the people who loved her — the exact date she came into the world.

September 1870 · Bellville, Austin County, Texas Born Free

Hellen Nichols was born in September 1870 in Bellville, Austin County, Texas. She was the fifth child of Joseph Nichols and Easter Bell Nichols — born five years after Juneteenth, five years after her parents walked out of bondage into a world that had technically freed them but had not finished deciding what that meant.

Her mother Easter had been born enslaved on July 4, 1838 — on a plantation whose owner would die before he could see the end of what he had built. Her father Joseph had been born enslaved in that same household, recorded as Mulatto in every census after emancipation, his mother's name not known by any document that survives. They married on December 18, 1865 — six months after Juneteenth. They had already had three children by the time Hellen arrived.

Hellen was the first in her family born into freedom from the very first moment of her life. That is not a small thing. That is what her parents had waited their entire lives for.

The 1870 census — the same census that recorded her birth — shows her father Joseph as a farm laborer who could not read or write. The community around her in Bellville was a formerly enslaved community trying to build lives on the same land where they had been held in bondage five years earlier. They had no schools, little money, no political protection as Reconstruction collapsed. But they were free. And Hellen had never been anything else.

1870 — 1898 · Bellville, Austin County, Texas Growing Up in Bellville

The 1880 census confirms Hellen's presence in the Nichols household — approximately 10 years old, one of six children in the household with Joseph age 37 and Easter age 33. She grew up on the same land where her parents had been enslaved. The 2 acres that Easter and Joseph had acquired from the John Nichols Survey — Abstract 73 — was the family ground. The town of Bellville around her had been built on land that John Nichols had owned. The Bell family whose name was on the town was the same Bell family whose name Easter had carried as her own at emancipation.

Hellen's world was Bellville. She knew every family in that formerly enslaved community — the Armstrongs who had come from South Carolina and North Carolina, the Nichols families who had been enslaved alongside her parents, Henry Nichols and Emmeline who farmed beside her father Joseph on the same precinct land. She grew up surrounded by people who had survived the same thing her family had survived. She grew up and she stayed. She was approximately 28 years old when she married.

November 27, 1898 · Austin County, Texas John Armstrong

On November 27, 1898 — Hellen Nichols married John Armstrong in Austin County, Texas. The Armstrong family origin is confirmed by primary source death certificates. John's father was Dave Armstrong — born in South Carolina — who married Clara Morgan (born North Carolina ca. 1848) in Austin County Texas on August 29, 1868 (Marriage Record No. 376). Both of John's parents came from the Carolinas — the same origin geography as the Waldrop-Davenport enslaved community that produced Easter Bell Nichols. Dave Armstrong born South Carolina. Hellen's great-grandmother Patience had come from South Carolina to Texas through the Waldrop family. When John Armstrong married Hellen Nichols in 1898 — two families from the same South Carolina origin, separated by the slave trade and decades of bondage, came together again in Bellville, Texas.

John Armstrong ran a transfer line — a delivery wagon and trucking operation — in Bellville. He was a business owner. He and Hellen were building something.

Research Note · Louisa Morgan

Earlier research identified a woman named Louisa Morgan as Clara Morgan's mother — making her John Armstrong's maternal grandmother. That identification has not been confirmed by a primary source document. Clara Armstrong's 1918 death certificate (filed by her son John Armstrong) lists both parents as Unknown. The source for Louisa as Clara's mother needs to be verified before it can be treated as confirmed.

June 1900 · Bellville, Austin County, Texas Eva

By June 1900 — the first census after their marriage — the household was three. John Armstrong, age 22. Hellen Armstrong, age 23 per the census — born September 1870. And Eva Armstrong — 4 months old — born June 27, 1900.

The family was renting in 1900 — they did not yet own their home. But John had a business. He and Hellen had a daughter. They had four months of Eva when the census enumerator came through Bellville.

Hellen had two children in total. One did not survive to 1910. The name of that child has not been found in any primary source document. He or she was born between 1899 and 1910 — and died. Eva survived.

Open Research Thread · Unnamed Child

The 1910 census records Hellen as having 2 children born and 1 living. Eva Armstrong is the surviving child. One child was born and died between 1899 and 1910. No name, no birth certificate, no death certificate has been found. The child may have died in infancy before Texas death registration was complete in this community.

1910 · Fort Worth Ward 5, Tarrant County, Texas Fort Worth

By 1910 the family had left Bellville and moved to Fort Worth — Ward 5, Tarrant County. John Armstrong was 28 years old. Eva was 10. Hellen — recorded as Helen Armstrong, age 25 in the census — had been married 11 years. The census recorded two children born and one living.

Note on Hellen's Recorded Age

The 1910 census records Helen Armstrong as born ca. 1885 — age 25. The 1870 census records her birth as September 1870, making her approximately 40 in 1910. The 1930 census records her as age 46, born ca. 1884. Her death certificate records her age as "about 70" in 1931 — implying ca. 1861. No primary source document records her actual birth date. Her daughter Eva, who was the informant on her 1931 death certificate, wrote "Don't Know" for date of birth. The 1870 census — the document closest to her actual birth — is the strongest evidence for her birth year. The later census ages are almost certainly underestimates.

August 30, 1911 · Fort Worth, Tarrant County, Texas The Night Jack Thompson Shot Her Husband Dead

On the evening of August 30, 1911 — at 6:30 p.m. — John Armstrong was shot and killed near the East Front Street entrance to the Terminal Hotel in Fort Worth. He was 32 years old. Hellen was approximately 41. Eva was 11. John had been driving a delivery wagon for the Denver News Service.

A white man named Jack Thompson — who operated a competing transfer line at 15th and Commerce — shot him. Thompson drove to Houston Street, met former police commissioner George H. Mulkey, and surrendered to police. Bond was set at $3,000. It was posted within hours by Clarence Parker, George W. Mulkey, and W.P. McLean Jr. — some of the most prominent white men in Fort Worth. Thompson went home that night.

Negro Truck Driver Is Shot and Killed Fort Worth Daily Newspaper · August 31, 1911

Jack Thompson — who operated a transfer line business at 15th and Commerce Streets — shot and killed John Armstrong, a negro truck driver, near the East Front Street entrance to the Terminal Hotel at 6:30 o'clock Wednesday evening. Thompson drove around the corner to Houston Street, met former police commissioner George H. Mulkey, and went to police headquarters to surrender. Justice Tom Maben fixed Thompson's bond at $3,000, which was promptly signed by several white bondsmen — Clarence Parker, George W. Mulkey, W.P. McLean Jr.

Thompson's defense was self-defense. A witness named Lon Sullins testified that John Armstrong had advanced on Thompson's buggy with a knife. The outcome of the trial has not been confirmed by primary source. The pattern is familiar — a Black man dead at close range, a white man free on bond within hours, signed by the city's most prominent white citizens.

Hellen Armstrong was widowed at approximately 41 years old with an 11-year-old daughter. She was alone in Fort Worth. The city where her husband had just been killed in the street.

1911 — 1930 · Fort Worth, Texas · Nineteen Unrecorded Years After

After John Armstrong's death in August 1911 there are nineteen years before Hellen appears again in the documentary record. The 1920 census has not located her under any confirmed name. She may have been living with Eva. She may have been working. She was in her forties — a widow in Jim Crow Fort Worth — with no husband, no confirmed income, and a daughter to finish raising.

At some point in those nineteen years she appears to have married at least twice more. By 1930 she is recorded as Helen Gilbert — widowed. By her death in December 1931 she is recorded as Helen Reese. Whether these represent two marriages, or one marriage and a census error, or some other explanation, has not been confirmed by primary source. No marriage record for either a Gilbert or Reese union has been found.

The Reese family is documented as a Black family in Bellville during this period — Louis L. Reese, Enoch Reese, and multiple Reese children all born in Bellville between 1895 and 1905. Albert Reese, born ca. 1880, is the most probable candidate for a husband. But this is hypothesis, not confirmed by document.

Open Research Thread · The Reese and Gilbert Names

Hellen Nichols Armstrong appears in the 1930 census as Helen Gilbert and dies in 1931 as Helen Reese. No marriage records for either a Gilbert or Reese husband have been located in Fort Worth, Tarrant County, or Austin County records from 1911 to 1931. The 1920 census has not been searched successfully for her. This is a genuine research gap. April 1930 · Fort Worth, Tarrant County, Texas 1121 Pulaski Avenue

In April 1930 — the last confirmed record before her death — Hellen Nichols Armstrong is living at 1121 Pulaski Avenue in Fort Worth with her daughter Eva and Eva's family. She is listed as Helen Gilbert. She is widowed. She is 46 years old per the census — born ca. 1884. The column for age at first marriage shows 15.

She was living with Eva. Margie Nell Ballard — who would become Margie Nell Ballard Corbin — was one year old in April 1930. Hellen Nichols Armstrong was in the same room as her granddaughter. She held Margie when Margie was an infant. That is confirmed. The 1930 census put them in the same house on Pulaski Avenue.

Sometime after April 1930 she went back to Bellville. To Easter Bell. To the town where she was born. To Jeff Cemetery, where she would be buried.

December 5, 1931 · 2:30 a.m. · Bellville, Austin County, Texas She Came Home to Die

Hellen Nichols Armstrong died at 2:30 in the morning on December 5, 1931 in Bellville, Texas. She had been sick since at least October 15 — Dr. O.E. Steck attended her for six weeks. He last saw her alive on November 25. She died ten days later in the middle of the night.

The cause of death was Pellagra — a disease caused by severe niacin deficiency. It was epidemic among poor Black communities in the Jim Crow South in the 1920s and 1930s. A disease of poverty. A disease that was preventable with adequate nutrition. She died of not having enough to eat, in the town where she had been born free sixty-one years earlier.

Eva Ballard was the informant on the death certificate. Eva — 31 years old, living in Fort Worth — had come back to Bellville for her mother's final weeks, or she had followed the news and come for the death. She stood in front of a clerk in Bellville on December 7, 1931 and gave what information she could.

The clerk asked for Hellen's date of birth. Eva said she didn't know. The clerk wrote: Don't Know.

Eva knew her mother's parents — Joe Nichols and Easter Nichols. She knew her mother had been born in Austin County Texas. She knew she was a widow and had been a housewife. But she did not know — and neither had anyone else around her — the exact date her mother was born. No document had ever recorded it while it mattered. The 1870 census — an enumerator passing through Bellville recording strangers — is the only surviving evidence that Hellen Nichols was born in September 1870.

She was buried at Jeff Cemetery the same day she died. December 5, 1931. In the same cemetery as Easter Bell Nichols — who would follow her daughter there four years later in December 1935. In the same community where both of them had been born. The first generation born free, buried in the ground of the town where her parents had been enslaved.

"Don't Know."

That is what Eva wrote on her mother's death certificate when the clerk asked for Hellen's date of birth. Not because Eva did not love her mother. Because no one had ever written it down while it mattered. The 1870 census enumerator — a stranger with a ledger — is the only person who preserved the fact that Hellen Nichols was born in September 1870. Her own daughter did not know it sixty-one years later.

Three Names · One Woman · Three Primary Sources She Is One Person

Hellen Nichols Armstrong went by multiple names because she married multiple times. The documentary record shows her as:

Hellen Nichols Armstrong — born Bellville 1870, married John Armstrong 1898, 1900–1910 censuses.

Helen Gilbert — 1930 census, 1121 Pulaski Avenue Fort Worth, widowed, mother of Eva Ballard.

Helen Reese — Death Certificate No. 54237, December 5, 1931, Bellville, Texas, parents Joe and Easter Nichols.

Three independent primary sources confirm she is one person. The death certificate names her parents as Joe and Easter Nichols. The 2006 Austin County tax foreclosure (Cause No. 97V-003) names her as a legal heir — "Helen Armstrong, Also Known As: Helen Gilbert" — in the same suit as her siblings Allen, Oscar, Clara, and Willey Nichols. And Find a Grave Memorial No. 199130041 places her at Jeff Cemetery in Bellville. She is one person. The names changed because life changed her circumstances. The identity did not change.
