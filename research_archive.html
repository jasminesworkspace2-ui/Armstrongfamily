Research Archive.

The family-story pages tell this history the way it should be told — as a story. This page is what sits underneath it: every record, every evidence tier, every unresolved name, every rule for what a document can and cannot prove. Nothing here is a hypothesis dressed up as a fact.

Core Rules

An appraiser, witness, administrator, or executor is not automatically an owner. Same-name people are never merged without a documentary continuity bridge. Matching surnames, ages, values, geography, or DNA alone are never treated as proof. The Legal Ownership Network (who held whom, on paper) is tracked separately from the Biological/Family Network (who was actually related to whom).

Molley & the Early Sims Records

Documented
1793 Union County Deed Book B:467 · March 22, 1793
James Sims Sr. → Peter Braselman · Mortgage, £1,000 sterling
Mortgaged 600 acres, a 150-acre Tyger River tract, and numerous enslaved people, named in family groups: Jack and Doll with Ambrose and Paul; Isaac and Tabby with Charles, Edward, Delf, Abram, Sawny, Tom, and Patience; Big Lucy and Daniel; Tabby, Peter, Delsey, Major, and Lucy; Jenny with Mary, Henry, Peter, Delsey, and children Morning, Daniel, Isaac, Lucy, Amy, Frank, and Doll.
What It ProvesThis places Patience in James Sims Sr.'s enslaved property network in 1793.
What It Does Not ProveHer mother, her father, her later owner, or any biological relationship to Molley.
Unresolved
James Sims Sr.'s Will · Nov. 20, 1794 · Proved Jan. 1, 1795
Union County Will Book A, p. 34
Wife Elizabeth, executrix; Peter Brasellmann and John Sanders, executors. Children: Drusilla/Drucilla Brasellmann, Anne G. Sims, Matthew, John, Nathan, Reuben, James. Anne G. Sims received Tabb and five children: Milley, Fanney, Dilley, Robin, Peter. Patience is not named in this will. Her disposition after the 1793 mortgage remains unresolved. OCR readings resembling "Jeter Brasellmann" are errors — no such person exists.
Documented
June 26, 1793 · Union Book B:466
James Sims Sr. → Drucilla Braselman
Nine enslaved people: Phillis, Sarah, Ithea, Stephen, Benjamin, Abina, Barlet, Susannah, Henry.
Unresolved
1790 Newberry Census
David Sims (7 enslaved), Matthew Sims (24), Reuben Sims (8), William Simms (1), Peter Brasilmon (4), James Waldroop (3). Which Reuben Sims this is, and his relationship to James Sims Sr., is unresolved.
03

Patience & the Waldrop/Davenport Connection

Documented
Newberry Deed Book C:367–368 · August 4, 1795
Francis Davenport Sr. → Patty Waldrop · Witnesses: Isaac Davenport, Edward Turner
Conveyed one Negro girl named Patience. Proved by Isaac Davenport, August 5, before Charles Griffin, J.P. Recorded August 18. The phrase "with her increase" comes from the later 1839 deed's own recital — not this original 1795 document — unless a clearer image of the original proves otherwise.
Documented
Newberry Deed Book C:369 · Same Day
Francis Davenport Sr. → Nancy Waldrop
One enslaved girl, Eliza — a separate transaction, separate recipient, same day.
Supported
Martha "Patsy" Davenport = Patty Waldrop
Compiled Geneanet material identifies Martha Patsy Davenport as daughter of Francis Davenport and Ann Whaley. An independent OBCGS Modified Register states Hezekiah Waldrop married Martha "Patsy" Davenport in 1791, identifying her as Francis Davenport's daughter. Treated as strongly supported pending a primary parentage record.
Documented
Samuel Waldrop's 1799 Will
Independently identifies James Davenport as Paty Waldrop's own grandfather — a documented legal link between the two families. This is a different Paty from Martha "Patsy" Davenport Waldrop.
Documented
1827 Hezekiah Waldrop → Stephen R. Waldrop Transfer
Names Patience, Rachel, and Thomas. Clarissa is not named in this specific transfer.
OCR Needs Verification
1839 Rankin County Deed Reconstruction
Reports the 1795 deed gave Patty Waldrop "Patience with her increase," held in trust by E.P. Waldrop after Patty's death, then transferred to Patience, Rachel, Clarissa, Tom (Rachel's son), William, Amanda, and Harriet (Clarissa's children) — distributed to Claiborne P. Waldrop, Fanny Nichols, James D. Waldrop, Stephen R. Waldrop, Wiley B. Waldrop, Patsy Stoddard, Sarah Robinson, Selina Robinson.
Hypothesis
Molley — Mother or Sister to Patience?
Whether Molley was Patience's mother or her sister remains genuinely unresolved. Any biological relationship between Patience's children and Waldrop men is a hypothesis, not a documented fact, unless directly supported by a primary source.

Waldrop Family Research

A recurring Waldrop–Pitts–Griffin–Henderson–Golding–Herndon–Davenport legal/property network runs through Newberry County. Repeated probate participation does not itself establish ownership.

OCR Needs Verification
James Waldrop Sr. · 1798/1799
Newberry Will Book B, p. 63 · Box 21, Package 6
Compiled material reports Jack, Mary, and Cloe transactions — unverified against the original packet. His 1790 household reportedly held three enslaved people; not assumed to be these same three.
Strong Research Lead
James Waldrop Jr. · 1799 — The $390 Woman
His own inventory lists no enslaved person. A separate sale bill reads "Mary Waldrop — a negro wench — $390." Documented: a $390 woman appears, associated with the name Mary Waldrop. Not established: that James Jr. owned her, or that her own name was Mary.
Unresolved
John Milligan Estate · 1799
Jane Milligan, administratrix; James Laffan, John Waldrop, Robert Blake, appraisers. One enslaved man, $300. John Waldrop's appraiser role does not establish ownership.
Documented
Samuel Waldrop · 1799
Names Susan, Paty, and an unborn child; identifies James Davenport as Paty's grandfather. Supplied inventory does not establish slaveholding at death.
OCR Needs Verification
Stephen Waldrop · 1816
Mary Waldrop and Elisha Golding, administrators. Inventory contains real enslaved-property evidence — OCR reads approximately "$108 Negro man Naund, Moses $50." Neither name is confirmed against the original image. Recurring Waldrop, Pitts, Golding, Herndon names appear in the sale bill, but garbled OCR is never published as settled fact.
Earlier Land Records
1761 John Earl Granville → John Waldrop (480 acres); 1764 John Waldrop & wife Millicent → Samuel Snead; 1770 John Waldrop → John Fullilove (200 acres); 1770/71 Harris → Waldrop (140 acres); 1790 Frederick Fraser → James Waldrop (550 acres); 1797/1801 David Waldrop's share → Jehu Pitts, with Mary Waldrop's dower release. Property-network evidence — not automatic biological links.

Nichols / Texas Research

Documented
Fanny's Identity — Treat as Settled
Nancy Jane Francis "Fanny" Waldrop = Fanny Waldrop = Frances Jane Waldroup = Fanny Nichols — all the same person. Daughter of Hezekiah Parrish Waldrop and Martha "Patsy" Davenport; later wife of John Nichols of Bellville, Texas. DNA correspondence corroborates; it does not independently prove.
Unresolved
1860 Bellville Slave Schedule — Nine People
John Nichols's own household. No individual in this schedule is identified as Easter, Rendy, or any other named ancestor without a continuity bridge. Age-matching alone is not sufficient.

Do Not Merge: Newberry Nichols (Solomon Nichols's own records — Bett, Prince, Dick, Sarah, Belle) is a separate research track from Texas Nichols. Newberry Bell is likewise separate from Texas Bell. Neither is connected without a documentary bridge.

07

Easter Research — Two Separate Women

Documented — Confirmed Separate
Easter #1 — John Pringle's Easter
Laurens District · May 7, 1817
Pringle emancipated a Black woman named Easter, stating she had been with him about 28 years. Described as about 40 years old, five feet tall, of good character, capable of earning a livelihood. Approximate birth: 1777. Confirmed as a separate record — not merged with the Davenport/Burton Easter below.
Strong Research Lead
Easter #2 — Davenport/Burton Easter
Dec. 11, 1792: Francis Davenport → James Davenport, one enslaved girl named Easter — establishing an Easter in the Davenport network. Dec. 29, 1829, William Burton estate sale: Jonathan Davenport purchased Easter ($80) and Bluford ($375); the same sale bill lists a separate Bluford purchased by William Burton for $215. Jonathan Davenport's 1842 will identifies Bluford directly as "son of Easter." The 1792 Easter → 1829 Easter continuity is unresolved — the 1829/1842 Easter-Bluford unit is itself well documented.

Do Not Merge: the 1792 Davenport Easter and the 1829/1842 Easter, without documented continuity. John Pringle's 1817 Easter and the Davenport/Burton Easter. "Esther" in Jonathan Davenport's 1842 will (old Sam's wife) and Easter — these are two different women.

Legal Ownership Network vs. Biological Network

Legal Ownership Network — Confirmed Transactions
Francis Davenport Sr. → Patty Waldrop: Patience (1795) · Francis Davenport Sr. → Nancy Waldrop: Eliza (1795) · Francis Davenport Sr. → Francis Davenport Jr.: Anthony (1795) · Owen Flinn → Isaac Davenport: Milly, Selay, Jack (1798) · William Burton estate → Jonathan Davenport: Easter, Bluford (1829) · Jonathan Davenport → James M. Davenport: Bluford (1842) · James Sims Sr. → Drucilla Braselman: nine people (1793).
Biological Network Signal
What's Reasoned, Not Proven
Patience's later family group per the 1839 reconstruction: Rachel, Clarissa, Tom, William, Amanda, Harriet. Jonathan Davenport's 1842 will directly establishes Kit→Sam, Martha+Anderson→children, Bluford→son of Easter, Albert→Nelson, Dorcas→Ann/Peggy. Fanny Waldrop is established as Hezekiah's daughter and later Nichols's wife. DNA is corroboration, not sole proof, of any specific enslaver/enslaved relationship.

Central working question: what biological family network existed underneath the legal ownership network connecting the Sims, Davenport, Waldrop, and Nichols families?

Do-Not-Merge List

John Pringle's 1817 Easter ≠ Davenport/Burton Easter, unless a bridge is found.

1792 Davenport Easter ≠ 1829/1842 Easter, until continuity is documented.

1829 Jonathan Davenport's Bluford ($375) ≠ 1829 William Burton's Bluford ($215), without continuity.

The $390 unnamed woman ≠ Jack, Mary, or Cloe, without direct evidence.

The $300 Milligan man ≠ John Waldrop's own property, merely because John Waldrop appraised him.

Stephen Waldrop's 1816 people ≠ the 1799 Waldrop people, without continuity.

Newberry Nichols ≠ Texas Nichols, without a documentary bridge.

Newberry Bell ≠ Texas Bell, without a documentary bridge.

Esther, in Jonathan Davenport's 1842 will, ≠ Easter.

Same-name Davenports are never automatically merged.

The minor Patsy Davenport named in the 1817 guardianship record ≠ Patty/Patsy Waldrop, without continuity.

A DNA match is never treated as proof of a specific enslaver/enslaved relationship.

Next Research Phase

The next collection phase gathers Newberry slave bills of sale directly, including transactions where enslaved people carry no surname at all. Each one will record: date, seller, buyer, the enslaved person's given name exactly as written, sex and age, family relationships, price, place, witnesses, book/page/package/image reference, transcription, what the document proves, what it does not prove, continuity clues, and its own evidence status. No enslaved person is ever identified with another merely because a given name matches.
