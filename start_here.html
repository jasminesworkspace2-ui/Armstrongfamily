In Plain Language · What This Archive Is

In 1795 a man named Francis Davenport gave his daughter Patty Waldrop an enslaved girl named Patience in Newberry County, South Carolina. That transaction was recorded in a deed book. That deed book survived. Because of that document — and hundreds of others found over seven years of research — we can trace Patience's descendants by name from 1795 all the way to today.

Patience had a daughter named Clarissa. Clarissa was the mother of Easter Bell Nichols — born enslaved on July 4 1838 in Bellville, Texas. Easter Bell lived 97 years. She had a daughter named Hellen. Hellen had a daughter named Eva. Eva had a daughter named Margie. Margie had a daughter named Hjordis. Hjordis had a daughter named Jasmine — who built this archive.

Nine generations. One unbroken line. All confirmed by primary source documents — deeds, death certificates, census records, voter registrations, slave schedules, and court filings. No guessing. No family tree websites. Only documents.

The Family Stories · One Page Per Person

Patience ca. 1765 The oldest confirmed ancestor. Born ca. 1765. Given as property to Patty Waldrop in Newberry County South Carolina on August 4 1795 — confirmed by a primary source deed (Deed Book C: 367–368). Her story begins the chain that leads to every person in this archive. Molley Before Patience The ancestor before Patience. Documented in Union County South Carolina in 1793. Her origin traces to the West African Bight of Benin — consistent with the Rainbow 1758 transatlantic voyage confirmed in the slave trade database. She is the bridge to Africa. Clarissa ca. 1807 Patience's daughter. Confirmed in South Carolina and Mississippi deed records. She traveled with the Waldrop family from South Carolina to Mississippi — documented in the 1839 Rankin County Mississippi deed that distributed her to a new household. Rendy Blayn ca. 1817 Easter Bell's mother. Confirmed in the 1860 John Nichols slave schedule as a Black female age 43. Her surname Blayn traces to the Blain/Bland enslaving family of Fayette County Texas — adjacent to Austin County where she was held. Born in Arkansas or North Carolina — the two census records conflict. Easter Bell Nichols 1838–1935 Born enslaved July 4 1838 in Bellville Texas. Died December 17 1935 age 97. Held legal title to a piece of the John Nichols Survey land — the same land she was born enslaved on — for 140 years after emancipation. The land was foreclosed by Austin County in 2006. Her daughter Hellen is the direct ancestor of this family's living descendants. Hellen Nichols Armstrong 1870–1931 Easter Bell's daughter. Born September 1870 in Bellville. Died December 5 1931. Confirmed as Helen Armstrong and Helen Gilbert by three independent primary sources — her death certificate, a cemetery record, and a 2006 court filing. She married twice — first to John Armstrong, then to Albert Reese after John Armstrong's murder in 1911. Eva Armstrong Ballard ca. 1899 Hellen's daughter. Married James Wilson Ballard Sr. Eva's story connects the Armstrong/Nichols line from Bellville Texas to the Ballard family line documented from Newton Ballard through Rev. George W. Ballard. Margie Nell Ballard Corbin 1929 Eva's daughter. Married Norman Corbin. Margie's story bridges the Texas Ballard family to the next generation. Hjordis L. Corbin 1960 Margie's daughter. The 8th generation in the confirmed chain. AncestryDNA kit SQ8499767. Her DNA confirms the maternal line connection to every ancestor documented in this archive. Joseph Nichols ca. 1843 Easter Bell's husband. Born ca. 1843 in Bellville Texas. Registered to vote on January 27 1868 in Bellville — less than three years after emancipation. Married Easter Bell December 23 1869. Their children held the John Nichols Survey land for 140 years.

The Ballard Family Line

Newton Ballard The earliest confirmed Ballard ancestor. Appears in the 1850 Smith County Texas census and on Rev. George W. Ballard's 1937 death certificate as "father, no record of birthplace." His origins are unresolvable from existing records — the death certificate permanently closes the search for his birthplace. Ballard Family History The complete Ballard family documentation — Newton Ballard through Rev. George W. Ballard through James Wilson Ballard Sr. through Eva Armstrong Ballard. Includes the Union Bank debt records, the 1840 Grand Gulf Bank deed naming six enslaved people, and the Fayette County Texas connections. Ballard Research Tracker A running record of every confirmed source for the Ballard line and every open research question. Updated as new documents are found. Useful for researchers who want to know exactly what has been confirmed and what remains open.

The Bellville Community · The People Around Them

John Nichols · Enslaved Community New 2026 A reconstruction of every person held in the John Nichols household in Bellville from 1827 through 1864 — drawn from the 1860 slave schedule, the Stephen F. Austin Register of Families, and post-emancipation census records. Includes the 2006 and 2014 tax foreclosures that took the land. Includes Emeline Nickols — the oldest confirmed survivor of the household — still living in Bellville in 1930 at age 83. Also documents the Waldrop family connection confirmed across three states. Freedom What happened after emancipation in Bellville. The community that was enslaved together stayed together — documented in census records from 1870 through 1940 in the same Bellville neighborhood. Includes voter registrations, land ownership records, and the post-emancipation lives of Easter Bell's community. Voices Primary source quotes — the words of people who were there. Includes WPA slave narratives from the 1930s, newspaper accounts from the Bellville Countryman, and the Katie Darling interview documenting conditions in Austin County during slavery.
