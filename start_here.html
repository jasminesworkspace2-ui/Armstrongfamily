In Plain Language · What This Archive Is

In 1795 a man named Francis Davenport gave his daughter Patty Waldrop an enslaved girl named Patience in Newberry County, South Carolina. That transaction was recorded in a deed book. That deed book survived. Because of that document — and hundreds of others found over seven years of research — we can trace Patience's descendants by name from 1795 all the way to today.

Patience had a daughter named Clarissa. Clarissa was the mother of Easter Bell Nichols — born enslaved on July 4 1838 in Bellville, Texas. Easter Bell lived 97 years. She had a daughter named Hellen. Hellen had a daughter named Eva. Eva had a daughter named Margie. Margie had a daughter named Hjordis. Hjordis had a daughter named Jasmine — who built this archive.

Nine generations. One unbroken line. All confirmed by primary source documents — deeds, death certificates, census records, voter registrations, slave schedules, and court filings. No guessing. No family tree websites. Only documents.
