Every Named and Unnamed Enslaved Person · Confirmed by Primary Source

For Family Members · Plain Language

This is a list of every person confirmed in this research — both ancestors in the direct line and other enslaved people found in the same historical records. If you are searching for a family member and you know they may have been in Bellville, Texas or Newberry County, South Carolina — look here first. Every entry has a source so you can verify it yourself.

This registry documents every enslaved person confirmed in the primary source research for this archive. It is offered as a public resource to other researchers searching for their own ancestors. Every entry is confirmed by a primary source document. Nothing is invented or inferred.

Enslaved people were systematically excluded from the official record. Their names — when recorded at all — appear in the documents of the people who enslaved them: slave schedules, estate inventories, probate partitions, deed transfers, and church histories. This registry consolidates every such record found in the course of building this archive.

If your ancestor was enslaved in Austin County Texas, Smith County Texas, Upshur County Texas, Copiah County Mississippi, Washington County Mississippi, Wilcox County Georgia, Halifax County Virginia, or Newberry County South Carolina — this registry may help you find them. Every document cited here is accessible through FamilySearch, the Texas GLO, Ancestry, or the county courthouses listed in the Paper Trail.

Highlighted rows indicate confirmed ancestors of this family. All others are members of the same enslaved communities — people whose descendants may be searching for them.

A Note to Other Researchers

If you are searching for an ancestor who may have been enslaved in one of these communities — Austin County Texas, Smith County Texas, Upshur County Texas, Halifax County Virginia, Newberry County South Carolina, Wilcox County Georgia, or Copiah County Mississippi — this registry may help you locate which estate records to search. Every source cited here is accessible through FamilySearch, the Texas General Land Office, Ancestry.com, or the county courthouses.

If you find a connection to your own research, the compiler of this archive welcomes contact: Jasmine M. Lee · jlee@dockellisfoundation.com · WikiTree: Lee-59275
