Justice Precinct 1 · Bellville, Austin County, Texas · 1880–1940

The Elders of Bellville.

The oldest documented people in this community, side by side. Two of them were born in Africa. Several were born before the American Revolution. All of them lived to see freedom, in the same small precinct where your own ancestors were enslaved and later built their lives. This page holds their names, their ages, and the families that grew up around them.

A Note on Ages and Birthplaces

Almost none of the ages, birth years, or birthplaces on this page agree perfectly across records. The same person is often recorded as born in two, three, even four different states across different census years, and their age frequently shifts by five, ten, or twenty years from one decade to the next. This is not a sign of error, confusion, or a different person — it is the ordinary, expected condition of records for people who were born enslaved. Enslaved people were rarely told their own birth date, and enumerators, often speaking to whoever answered the door rather than the individual themselves, frequently guessed. A birth year moving from 1780 to 1800, or a birthplace shifting from Georgia to Texas, does not disqualify a match or a family connection. It is simply what the record of slavery looks like. Every identification on this page is made by weighing the whole pattern — age, household, neighbors, and later continuity — not by demanding one number match exactly.

Born in Africa
Jack Jackson Born Africa Born ca. 1780, Africa · Age 100 in 1880 · Justice Precinct 1
Head of household, married to Gabriella Jackson, with sons James and John Jackson in the home. Occupation recorded as Farmer at age 100. His age is almost certainly a rounded estimate rather than a precise count — but the stated birthplace, Africa, was not something an enumerator would typically guess at random. This is one of the most direct living links to the Middle Passage documented in this entire community.
Solomon Blake Born Africa Born ca. 1780, Africa · Age 100 in 1880 · Justice Precinct 1
Listed in the household with Mettie and Josephine Menke. Like Jack Jackson, recorded at exactly age 100 — the same rounding pattern — with Africa stated plainly as his birthplace.
Jim George Father Born Africa Born ca. 1763, South Carolina · Age 117 in 1880 · married to Fannie George
Already documented on the Registry page. Jim George's own father — and separately, his wife Fannie's father — are both recorded with birthplace Africa on the 1880 census. Jim's own recorded age of 117 is not a literal fact; it reflects how extreme old age was estimated for people whose birth was never formally recorded. Fannie George is a strong candidate for "Fanny," age 55 in the 1864 John Nichols estate partition.

Family Clusters of This Community
The Lott Family At least six distinct household clusters, 1880–1940
The largest single-surname family documented in this precinct. Ned Lott (b. ca. 1820, Georgia) is the oldest identified Lott, living as "Uncle" in Joe and Maria Lott's household in 1900, and appearing again in 1920 at age 97. Separate, apparently unrelated Lott households — headed by George W. Lott (b. 1830, Mississippi), Jacob Lott (b. 1853, Mississippi), Sam Lott (b. 1825, Virginia), and Joseph Lott (b. 1850, Mississippi) — suggest either several independent Lott families converging on Bellville from different origin states, or one extended family separated by the domestic slave trade and reunited only partially in Texas. Ennis Lott's own line is traceable continuously from 1910 through 1940, the clearest multi-generational thread in the whole family.
The Reese Family Over 100 individuals documented, 1880–1940 · not currently connected to the direct Nichols-Armstrong bloodline
Stack Reese (b. ca. 1790, Texas) anchors the oldest generation, with wife Amanda (b. 1826, Georgia) and children Perressie, Waller, and Ratience. A separate, larger branch descends from Frank Reese and Enoch Reese (both b. 1856, Alabama) — brothers or close relatives who each raised large families in the same precinct. This is a genuinely distinct, extensively documented Bellville family in its own right. No document yet connects this Reese family by blood to Helen Nichols Reese, the Nichols-family daughter who is separately documented on this site.
The Nicholson / Nickelson Family 1920–1940 · a distinct family from the Nichols line
Will and Mary Nickelson, and the related Nicholson households (Willie, Mary, Lulu), form their own family cluster. The surname's similarity to Nichols is coincidental — no shared household, parent, or document connects the two families.
Jefferson Nagy & the Nazy Family Jefferson Nagy b. ca. 1811, Louisiana — one of the oldest documented residents
Jefferson Nagy, recorded at age 89 in 1900, lived in the same household as Ned Bell. Separately, John Nazy and Clara Nazy (likely the same couple as your confirmed John Nazy and Clara Nichols, married 1891) appear here as "Nagy," with a son recorded as Roy Thompson — very likely Roy Nazy under a stepfather's or earlier surname.
How These Families Intertwine
Shared Households, Not Just Shared Surnames

Rosa Lott's household includes Luther Armstrong, grandson, born March 1897 — connecting the Lott and Armstrong families directly, likely through a daughter's marriage not otherwise documented. Ned Bell's household includes Jefferson Nagy with no stated blood relation — two elderly men from different origin states (South Carolina and Louisiana) sharing a home, the kind of mutual support common among elderly formerly enslaved people with no surviving family nearby. Fanny Nichols's later household (1910) includes an "adopted daughter" and "adopted granddaughter" with no blood tie at all — a reminder that family in this community was built through care and proximity as often as through blood, especially for those separated from their birth families by sale.

The Jail Enumeration Pattern

Several elderly residents — Ned Lott in 1920, and Joseph Nichols's own household in 1900 — appear with the relationship label "Inmate," a pattern already confirmed elsewhere on this site as a page-formatting artifact from the county jail's position on the enumerator's route, not an indication of actual incarceration.

The Complete 1860 Bellville Slave Schedule

Every enslaver and every enslaved person recorded in Bellville proper on the 1860 U.S. Federal Census Slave Schedule — 220 individual records across roughly 30 households. John Nichols's own household of 9 is the anchor point for this entire archive. The two largest households in town — Jos. S.R. Blake's 24 people and Wm. E. Crump's 20 — dwarf it. No age is a name, but every age here is a real person who lived, worked, and was held in this specific town.
